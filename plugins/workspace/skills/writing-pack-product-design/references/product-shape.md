# Design backward from what the buyer walks away with

> Reference for writing-pack-product-design. Read before modules, lessons, resources, the standalone course, and the download shape.

Start with what the buyer will be able to do and what they'll have made. Choose the evidence that shows it, then the instruction and practice that get them there. A finished reading checklist proves nothing. (Grounding: backward design — learning objectives first, then evidence, then instruction.)

## The module map

A module is a deliverable, named by what it produces: "Domain of Mastery", not "Introduction and Fundamentals". For each module record: the starting requirement, the decision or skill learned, what the buyer makes, the exercise, what the AI does, how to check it. Work backward until the first module starts at the buyer's real ability. Sequence prerequisites, not everything the creator knows. Cut what serves no step; advanced depth becomes a resource. No fixed module count, lesson length, or reading time.

## One lesson

Every lesson is a document whose first line is its title. Then:

1. **The idea**, in the creator's voice, connected to the last step. One central idea per lesson.
2. **One worked example**: the decision, the work, the reason for the choices. The creator's own material where it exists; a labeled case where it doesn't.
3. **Do This Now**: numbered, concrete, doable today, on the buyer's own material. "Find 3 tracks that stopped you this week. Save them. Write why each one worked." Not "reflect on your goals." When a step happens in another tool or page, link it so it opens in a new tab; never say "go to X" without the link.
4. **Do It With The AI**, only when the buyer uses the AI to make something in this lesson (a draft, a plan, research, a filled template). Skip it on setup, mindset, and reading lessons; a filler ask ("what will I build?") is padding. When it's there: the exact ask in quotes, plus what comes back and what the buyer does with it. "Ask the chat: 'Coach these posts. Hook 1 to 5, the angle, where the energy drops, one thing to try. Don't rewrite them.'" This ask becomes a job in the product AI's instructions, word for word in spirit.
5. **Next**: one line on what the next lesson gives them.

A lesson can teach an idea and produce a deliverable at once. An experienced buyer should be able to enter at their task without reading everything first.

Format: one block per line, no blank spacer lines, no em dashes, images one per line. Numbers and named things over adjectives. Short sentences.

## Help and transfer

Use explanation, demonstration, guided practice, feedback, and independent application where the goal needs them. If the buyer asks for a draft and the product promises drafting, draft it and explain the choices. Refusing to help is not teaching. To check learning, ask for a fresh example, a revision, or an explanation in the buyer's words. Separate work quality from learning quality: a good generated newsletter shows output quality; a buyer who can fix a weak argument on a new topic shows transfer.

## The tool shape

When the value is one repeatable job (a strategy, a script, a plan, a review), do not build lessons. Build: the job's process in steps; an artifact contract that fixes the output document's title pattern, section order, what each section must contain, and a length ceiling; a quality rubric the AI applies before showing anything; two or three worked examples for different starting points; and an activation doc that turns the artifact into a first month of action. Each is a short knowledge doc, two to four thousand characters. The whole product is the AI plus those docs. Judge it by one question: does the buyer leave the first chat with the artifact, in their words, ready to use?

## Resources

Templates, document shapes, checklists, swipe files, trackers, and calendars are Resources, consulted on demand, never lessons. The strongest resources are ones the AI writes into: a table of topics the AI fills after the interview, a template the AI completes with the buyer, a set of examples the AI quotes. A resource with no AI job attached is a download; a resource with one is part of the product. A template ships with sample rows so the buyer sees what it's for; the AI writes into the buyer's own copy, creates one when only the template exists, and never reads the template's sample rows as the buyer's real data or plan.

## The standalone course (no AI)

A course is preferably an AI course: chat on, lessons in order, and the AI coaching on whatever lesson is open. Only when the creator explicitly wants no AI, or is selling finished written material as is, ship the same course without it. Buyers read the lessons in order, copy and download them. The module map, lesson structure, and resources are the same; there is no AI job, no instructions, no starters, and the Do It With The AI step is dropped. An AI can be added later without moving anything, so build the lessons so an AI could coach on them later. A standalone course is not a download: a download is files only, with no lessons.

## The download shape

When the value is the files themselves and the buyer needs no help using them, sell a digital download: no lessons, no AI. What ships: the files, complete and named for the buyer ("Hook Swipe File, 50 hooks", not "hooks_v3_final"), in the order they are used; a folder or board when several documents belong together; a Claude skill as a zip of its folder; and a welcome page that is the whole onboarding: what each file is, what to do first, what they will have after, where the files live (under 300 words). Ask before building: are the files finished, does the buyer get a result from the files alone, and would an AI job make the result more likely? If the last answer is yes and the creator has time, build the AI product instead; a download can gain an AI later without rebuilding. A free download is a lead magnet whose welcome page points at the paid product.

## First result and the welcome page

Give a beginner one first task with a visible result, an example input, and a short version for a busy day. The welcome page tells them what they'll have after each module, how to go through it, what the AI does and doesn't do, where the resources are, and exactly where to start.

When converting a course, keep its strong explanations and examples, remove dead platform mechanics only after checking what replaces them, and link each old source to its new lesson or resource so nothing is silently dropped.
