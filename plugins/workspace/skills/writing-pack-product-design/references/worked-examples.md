# Worked examples: a shipped course and a shipped tool

> Reference for writing-pack-product-design. Read when you need to see a finished course and a finished tool. Use their SHAPE — never their words, story, topic, or results.

# Worked example 1, a course: The 2 Hour Writer

A real course product by Dan Koe, sold online with an AI built in. Everything below describes the product as shipped.

## The offer

Buyer: someone with skills and interests who wants to write online, or already does and asks "what do I post" every morning. Promise: one hour of writing a day (30 minutes on a newsletter, 30 on the posts that come from it) plus one hour that feeds it; one newsletter a week that becomes every post. Shape: course with an AI. Price: one-time. Proof: the creator's own audience and sales, and testimonials from the earlier version of the course.

## The module map (a module is a deliverable)

- Start Here: how the course works, the two tools, the weekly workflow.
- Domain of Mastery: what you write about, who you write to, a profile written in 30 minutes.
- Intelligent Imitation: capture ideas, make them yours, find what's already proven.
- Unlimited Posts: attention, the 7 angles, the structures, a one-hour writing gym, breaking down great writing.
- The Cornerstone Newsletter: the frameworks, topic and outline, writing it across the week, the content ecosystem.
- Grow It And Run It: four growth strategies and the weekly system that runs in 2 hours.
- The AI: what AI should and shouldn't write, and the index of every ask in the course.

21 lessons total. Every module ends with something the buyer has made: a note, a map, posts, a letter, a schedule.

## How one lesson ends (from "The Idea Capture System")

The lesson teaches the method with the creator's own story, then ends with three fixed blocks:

- **Do This Now** — four numbered steps doable today: find 2–3 idea-dense sources and save them; spend 15–20 minutes consuming one and capture at least 5 ideas through the lesson's filter, expanding each with the lesson's three-part prompt (Problem, Importance, Experience); spend 5–10 minutes reflecting without consuming and capture 3 more; open the capture chat with the AI and drop today's ideas into it.
- **Do It With The AI** — one exact ask in quotes, telling the AI to question each dropped idea, run the three-part expansion with the buyer, rate it 1–5 for their brand, name the strongest angle, and not write the post. Then: pin that chat; it is a thinking partner, not a ghostwriter, and it will tell you when an idea is a 2.
- **Next** — one line: tomorrow, how to make a captured idea yours.

## The matching job in the playbook

The same ask, written as behaviour, is step 1 ("Capture") of the Unlimited Ideas playbook, a knowledge doc the AI opens when that mode runs: read the dropped idea in full first; acknowledge it in one sentence and ask what made them save it before offering any reading of your own; fragments are the goal, never polish their phrasing; if they already said why, skip ahead; run the three-part expansion by asking, not listing, and never invent their Experience; rate it 1–5 for their brand and say why; name the strongest angle; never write the post.

Every lesson's ask is a step like this in one of four playbooks (Domain of Mastery, Unlimited Ideas, Unlimited Posts, Cornerstone Newsletter), each four to seven thousand characters, hidden from buyers. The instructions themselves are a 5,700-character router: who the AI is, the one rule it enforces (it does the parts of writing that aren't writing; the student writes the sentences), a table of the four modes with the lessons each retrieves and the playbook it opens, what it says on a first reply, and standing rules for material, memory, voice, evidence, handoffs, and scheduled runs. The newsletter mode opens with one question (what do you want to write about this week?) and reads nothing before the answer.

## Starters

Four, in the creator's own words, the first one starting the course: one walks the buyer through the course from lesson 1, one builds the Domain of Mastery by first asking what they'd talk about for years, one writes social posts using the course's structures, one outlines the newsletter by interviewing them for ideas.

## Resources (not lessons)

- Content Calendar: a table with sample rows. After the first interview the AI writes 12 weeks of newsletter topics and post ideas into the buyer's own copy (or creates one). It never reads the calendar to pick the week's letter, and never treats the template's sample rows as the buyer's plan.
- Daily Priorities: a table of the buyer's writing blocks, one recurring row per block.
- Master Swipe File: a board of real posts, each with a note on why it worked, plus a reference doc so the AI can quote the structures.
- Four templates the AI fills: the Domain of Mastery note, the Brand Strategy note, the Unique Perspective note, the Newsletter Outline note.

## The welcome page

Title, a warm opening in the creator's voice, "The system" in four lines, "What you'll have" after each module, "How to go through it" (go in order, do both asks before the next lesson, where lessons, chats, and notes live), "The AI" (the four modes with one starter each, and the rule), the resources, one line on commitment, and the last line: Start with Start Here.

## What's included — the shape

Seven one-line entries, each what it is plus why it matters; two are bonuses. Two of them are quoted as the canonical good examples in `copy.md` ("Know what to write about for the next 12 weeks after one 30-minute interview with the AI." and "Bonus: Content Calendar and Daily Priorities tracker to make accountability seamless."). The other five follow the same pattern for the course's foundations, its idea system, its writing workflow, growing without relying on social reach, and a bonus personal swipe file of the creator's top-performing content with breakdowns.

## Sales page headings — the shape

Seven title-case headings, each a confident claim in the creator's own voice: the cost of being good at something nobody knows about; the one skill every other skill depends on; one-person businesses getting it backwards; the most fulfilling two hours of the day; a pair that splits the work — "The Course Teaches You." / "The AI Writes With You."; and a closing promise about turning interests into an audience that pays. Read only those headings and the argument still holds; the section under each pays it off with the creator's own story and numbers.

# Worked example 2, a tool: Personal Brand Strategist

A shipped AI tool — and the source of `writing-pack-personal-brand`, which is this same shape. No lessons. One job: interview the person briefly, find the worldview that ties their work together, and write one strategy they can act on this month.

## The knowledge (nine short docs, two to four thousand characters each)

- The method: inside-out brand strategy, smallest viable audience, personal monopoly and earned credibility, desired associations, the topic tree.
- The artifact contract: one document titled "Personal Brand Strategy: <name>", sections in a fixed order (brand thesis and lens, smallest viable audience, personal monopoly and credibility, beliefs and tensions, topic tree, what to write about, initial post directions, first-month activation, inviting people into your worldview), a budget (about 1,000 words as a ceiling, no paragraph over two lines, omit any section with nothing real to say), and the rule that every correction updates this document instead of creating another.
- The quality rubric, applied silently before presenting: coherence, specificity, credibility, distinctiveness, sustainability, activation.
- First-month activation: a small portfolio of first pieces, a cadence the person can keep, and a qualitative evidence review.
- Worked examples for three starting points: a beginner with several interests, an experienced professional becoming visible, a client-work brand.

## The AI's instructions, in short

Who it is and the promise. Entry paths: material supplied (start from it), nothing supplied (a brief, story-first interview), an existing strategy supplied (refresh it in place). The interview asks for stories and facts, never self-assessment. Then it writes the strategy by the contract, checks it against the rubric, presents it, and revises the same document with every correction. It ends with the first month of action. It never invents proof, and it separates lived experience from study.

## Starters

Entry paths in the buyer's words: build my strategy, I have a rough idea to pressure-test, here is what I already have, refresh my existing strategy.

# What to copy from these

From the course: modules as deliverables, lessons that end in an action and an ask, an AI whose jobs are those asks, resources the AI fills, a welcome page that says what they'll have. From the tool: one artifact with a contract, a rubric the AI runs on itself, worked examples for different starting points, an activation handoff. From both: four starters, the creator's voice, nothing invented. Never the words, the story, or the topic.
