# The Product Builder method

> Reference for writing-pack-product-design. Read before the interview and the Product Brief.

The outcome is a useful, distinctive product with a believable offer. A long curriculum, a clever name, and an enthusiastic AI answer do not establish that outcome.

## Pick the shortest useful entry

- **No idea:** interview for a real result, recover the creator's decision process, research plausible buyers, compare three offers.
- **Chosen idea:** check audience, outcome, method, evidence, and delivery. Do not reopen settled positioning without a concrete reason.
- **Existing material:** read a representative sample in full, including one worked example and the current sales page. Map what exists to buyer milestones. Keep strong material; find the missing practice, feedback, or AI job.
- **Finished files to sell as they are:** a digital download. Confirm the buyer gets a result from the files alone, then go straight to the offer, the file order and names, the welcome page, and the sales page. Skip steps 3 and 4 below.
- **Finished written lessons to sell in order with no AI:** a standalone course. Recommend the AI course first when an AI job would make the result more likely; the creator decides. Then the offer, the module map, the welcome page, and the sales page. Skip step 4 below.
- **Existing product:** compare its promise, lessons, AI instructions, real outputs, welcome, and buyer feedback. Report the three biggest gaps and fix the first.
- **Narrow request:** revise that piece in context. A headline edit does not need a new offer interview.

## Sequence

1. Recover expertise and the buyer's problem. Output: case evidence and a provisional method.
2. Research alternatives and choose an offer. Output: buyer, observable outcome, reason to choose, shape, and the question to validate.
3. Design backward. Output: modules as deliverables, each with what the buyer learns, makes, and asks the AI.
4. Build one complete slice. Output: one lesson, its AI job, an example exchange, the buyer's output, a pass/fail check. Review method and voice here.
5. Assemble the product. Output: all lessons, resources, the product AI, the course structure, page and welcome drafts.
6. Test, revise, and prepare a pilot. Output: real test records, open assumptions, and the creator's release decision.

These are dependencies, not six chat turns. Move through settled decisions in one run. Keep making progress while a question is pending; do not guess a required decision.

## Interview for judgment

The creator is the source of their own experience and standards. Research can sharpen a method; it cannot invent the experience that makes it theirs.

Open with one real instance: who had which problem, what had they tried, what did the creator do, what changed? Invite a before/after, feedback, an old course, a consultation, a session, or a draft. Fragments are enough. Never ask the creator to name a niche, mechanism, or positioning statement before you understand their work.

Pick the next question from the last answer:

- **First signal:** What did you notice that made you start there?
- **Sequence:** What breaks if someone skips this step?
- **Contrast:** Show a weak and a strong example. Point to the exact difference.
- **Exception:** Who would you give different advice to, and why?
- **Failure:** When did this fail? What did you change?
- **Transfer:** What can a beginner repeat, and what still needs your judgment?
- **Proof:** Which result did you see, over what period, and what else could explain it?
- **Value:** Which part do people keep struggling to do without you?

At most three at a time. After a round, play the method back with one concrete example and let the creator correct it.

For each decision record: the signal, what it means, the action, why, the exception, an example. Then the checks that tell the creator the work is good. A useful AI applies those checks to new material and explains its call.

## Originality test

Could a competitor sell the same offer, lesson, or AI answer with only the name changed? If so, find the real difference: buyer context, inputs, decision order, quality criteria, access to examples, or delivery. Never invent an acronym to hide generic advice. Familiar principles applied to a specific situation through real judgment are a real product.

## Thin material

Someone who has never had a client may still have a useful personal process, research library, or tool. Say what is and is not established. Propose a narrow prototype. Do not cut the price just because proof is thin. Never turn a hypothetical into a testimonial. When a lesson needs a personal example the creator cannot supply, leave it marked incomplete and name what would complete it.

## The Product Brief

One document named `Product Brief: <name>`. Record where it lives and reuse it every session. Sections:

- Name, current stage, next action.
- Buyer, purchase trigger, starting ability, constraints, desired observable result, exclusions.
- The creator's cases, standards, decisions, exceptions, proof, and voice samples, each linked to its source.
- The method in steps. Mark creator statements, outside research, and proposed synthesis separately.
- Alternatives, what buyers know, what they've heard too often, their language, dated source links.
- Offer: shape, components, price rationale, support and usage assumptions, confirmed terms, open decisions.
- Module map: for each module, what the buyer learns, makes, asks the AI, and how to check it.
- AI jobs: teach, draft, review, or execute per job; first reply; outputs; sources; memory; boundaries.
- Links to lessons, resources, the product AI, the page draft, the welcome, tests, pilot feedback.
- Claims: claim, the capability behind it, proof, status. Unsupported promises stay here until resolved.
- Decisions, rejected directions, creator corrections, change history.

Keep credentials and unrelated personal material out (and nothing from the vault). Reread before every write; never overwrite a recent edit by the creator from memory.
