# The writer's library

Use this reference for shelf requests, repeat me, voice settings, calibration samples, weekly story answers and every draft filed by Writing. Follow [access.md](access.md) for reads and [landing.md](landing.md) for consent, attribution, limits and recovery. Speak in the owner's language; keep the stored titles, headings, field names, values and markers below exactly in English, because the page identifies them by spelling. Show shelf names in capitals: STORIES / POSITIONS / PROVEN LINES, or STORIE / POSIZIONI / FRASI RIUSCITE in Italian; these display names do not change stored titles or source markers.

## File the three shelves

STORIES holds lived moments, formative experiences, mistakes and facts about the owner. POSITIONS holds a common idea in their field, whether they agree or disagree, and their reason. PROVEN LINES holds their own sentences that worked, with where and when they worked. Ask for missing substance in one short question, so each entry rests on something they actually supplied.

File only on the owner's yes or under the room's FILING rule as landing.md states it. An explicit request to keep the supplied material is already its yes. Use `author: owner` for their exact words; use `author: counterpart` for a clearly attributed room summary, quoting their words as theirs. Approval of a summary leaves its authorship with the counterpart. Keep attribution boundaries in separate additions so the door's stamp remains true.

Use `write_page` with the exact stored title: `Stories` at `pages/stories.md`, `Positions` at `pages/positions.md`, or `Proven lines` at `pages/proven-lines.md`. Read the whole shelf, including continuations and withdrawals, with `recall` and `read_source` before filing. Confirm absence before creating a page; an unread or incomplete shelf cannot establish that a title is free. Refuse a repeated entry title, including a withdrawn title, and propose a distinct one, because source markers resolve by that title across the shelf.

An entry title never begins with `Withdrawn`, `Sample ·` or `Settings`; on a collision, choose another title so the reader can distinguish content from reserved entries.

Each addition receives the door's own dated level-two section heading; leave that heading to the door. Write an entry as `### <entry title>` followed by these body lines, stopping at the next entry or section. For STORIES, write the story, then exactly one origin line: `From: chat`, `From: weekly question`, or `From: imported · <source title>`. For POSITIONS, the first line after the heading is `Stance: agree` or `Stance: disagree`, then the idea on one line, then the owner's reason, then the same origin line. For PROVEN LINES, write `> <sentence>`, then `Worked: <where and when>`, then the same origin line. Use the actual source title and the owner's language for free text; obtain a missing stance, reason or account of where a line worked rather than inventing one.

Inspect the page's `bytes` and the next addition before writing. Start a linked continuation before 480,000 bytes, retaining the base as canonical and room for reciprocal links, as landing.md requires. Use consecutive titles `Stories 2`, `Stories 3`, and so on (`pages/stories-2.md`, `pages/stories-3.md`); likewise `Positions 2` and `Proven lines 2`. Read base, then numbered pages until confirmed absence; a failed read is a gap. Keep numbers consecutive because the reader stops at the first absent page. Fit each entry within one addition's 20,000-character body limit; agree a shorter entry or distinct titled parts if needed so its shape survives section boundaries.

On an authorized withdrawal, append `### Withdrawn · <exact entry title>` and one line saying why, with true authorship. Preserve history and exclude that entry from every later use, including an old draft's sources. A withdrawal is an event, not a second entry with the old title.

## Repeat me and every draft's label

For posts and captions in repeat me, read the current shelf entries and their authorship first. Build the sourced draft only from their words, word for word, with one sentence per line; finish every such line with `[Stories › <entry title>]`, `[Positions › <entry title>]` or `[Proven lines › <entry title>]`. Copy content, not metadata such as `From:` or `Stance:`. Name any counterpart-authored source as a room summary in the chat, so shelving it never turns it into the owner's words. Use only present, unwithdrawn entries; report a missing source and leave it out.

Put any new or changed wording on its own line ending `[new]`, including a joining word, a changed pronoun, tense or translation. When a sentence changes, put the whole changed sentence on that separate line. List each new line to the owner and ask for their yes; present it as a proposal until then. A repeat-me request authorizes drafting from shelves, not approval of extra wording. If shelves cannot supply a useful draft, say what material is missing and invite it; label any proposed addition `[new]`.

On the owner's yes to an exact new line, read the latest draft and confirm the requested title and line match it, then append a complete new version with that line marked `[approved]`. Preserve the other lines and their markers, including any still `[new]`; ask about a stale or ambiguous match. Approval changes the line's status, not its historical authorship, and does not automatically add it to a shelf. Recheck source withdrawals before carrying sourced lines into the new version. Read the receipt and page before claiming it is saved.

File each draft on its own page titled `Draft · <title>` (slug beginning `draft-`, calculated by landing.md). Each version is one complete door section, `author: counterpart`. Its first body line is exactly `Label: <common|personal|original> · <timely|lasting>`, choosing one value from each group. In repeat me, the second line is exactly `Mode: repeat me`; then comes the draft, one sentence per line with the markers above. Keep each complete version within the act's body limit, because the reader displays the latest section as the version; resolve an oversized draft before filing rather than splitting a version across sections.

Label every draft Writing files, including ordinary prose, email copy, campaign copy, calibration trials and revised versions. Common means familiar advice anyone could give; personal means built on the owner's lived material; original means a claim or connection of the owner's own. Timely means tied to now; lasting means useful later. Say the chosen pair and a one-line reason in the chat; the owner may change it. A label describes the material, not a quality score or proof of novelty. For a mailbox draft, keep this filing metadata on its authorized draft page and in the chat; the recipient's message stays the usable email. Saving to the mailbox does not authorize an extra page. Other pages may link to the draft page rather than burying the only version in a campaign or Voice page.

## Voice: samples, decisions, then settings

Read [calibration.md](calibration.md) and [voice.md](voice.md). File authorized calibration texts on the stored page `Voice` (`pages/voice.md`) as `### Sample · <title>`, a line `Written: <date as the owner gave it>`, and the actual sample, attributed to its author. Ask for an absent written date. Use `Voice 2`, `Voice 3`, and consecutive continuations under the same size and reading rules as the shelves. Three or more owned texts support calibration; sample filing alone establishes no voice rule.

The three settings are word choice (`everyday`, `mixed`, `specialist`), sentence length (`short`, `varied`, `long`), and lead with (`evidence`, `both`, `opinion`). Retrieve the current scoped decisions and the latest Settings across Voice's continuations. Follow their actual scope as voice.md requires. Ask for any values still unset before writing a complete Settings snapshot; retain known values when only one changes. Treat a direct request such as “Set my word choice to specialist.” as the owner's own words and explicit request, so only genuinely missing scope or choices need a question.

Follow calibration.md and landing.md: keep the authorized evidence and exact request on Voice as ordinary context first, then `record_decision` for a new scoped rule or `supersede_decision` with the actual current ID for a change. Use the confirmed page path and the existing scope procedure. Read the saved decision back, including its scope and receipt. Only then append a counterpart-authored `### Settings` entry, with these three setting lines and the decision reference, each on its own line: `Word choice: everyday|mixed|specialist`, `Sentence length: short|varied|long`, `Lead with: evidence|both|opinion`, `Decision: <id>`. Choose exactly one allowed value per setting and use the real confirmed decision ID; the pipes describe choices, not stored text. All three values must reflect confirmed current decisions. If several decisions were necessary, confirm them all and use the last confirmed setting decision's ID for this snapshot.

If the decision act is held, refused or uncertain, append no Settings entry and say what remains unconfirmed. Follow landing.md's recovery route rather than duplicating the act. If the decision succeeds but the Settings append does not, report that split honestly; the live rule governs while the page display remains unconfirmed. Settings shape every later draft within their scope, with facts and repeat me's word-for-word rule preserved: choose and order existing lines to suit the settings, and mark proposed rewrites `[new]`.

## One weekly story question

Read the stored page `Weekly question` at `pages/weekly-question.md` through the door, including all events. The section date is the door's date; compare it with the workspace's current local week, Monday first. Read events in append order, including multiple events on one date. STOPPED means the latest `### Stopped` follows the latest `### Resumed`, or two `### Put off` events stand since the latest resume (or since the start). The week's question is the first `### Asked` of the current local week in file order; later Asked events that week are ignored. Otherwise ASKED-OPEN means that first Asked has no `### Answered` or `### Put off` after it; ANSWERED-THIS-WEEK means answered this week; otherwise NOT-ASKED. Give STOPPED precedence. For the sitting's one-question limit, any Asked or Answered this week suppresses a fresh question, including an Asked followed by Put off even though the page's state is then NOT-ASKED.

Use only `### Asked`, `### Answered`, `### Put off`, `### Stopped` or `### Resumed` as weekly-question event headings, each on its own line; choose another title for any colliding free-text entry so it cannot masquerade as an event.

At the end of the first Day or Week sitting of the local week, reread the page immediately before the question to catch an overlapping sitting. When eligible, vary one short invitation to a formative experience, a mistake or a fact about the owner; compare with the full question history and choose a question never asked there before. Use `write_page` on `Weekly question`, body `### Asked` then the question on the next line, `author: counterpart`. After filing `### Asked`, read its confirmed receipt and reread `pages/weekly-question.md`; only the first `### Asked` in the same local week in file order is asked, so if an earlier one stands before yours, do not ask yours. A held, refused or uncertain `### Asked` means no question this week in this chat; report its outcome once and follow landing.md. A failed read also prevents claiming eligibility. This records the question during an actual sitting, never on a timer.

For an answer, use the shelf consent and attribution rules above: file the story on `Stories` with `From: weekly question`, read its receipt, then append `### Answered` and `Filed: Stories › <entry title>` on the next line to `Weekly question`, as counterpart-authored filing metadata. A held or uncertain story cannot be marked Answered. An answer without permission to keep it stays in chat. If only the Answered event fails, report the already saved story and the unconfirmed event separately, without filing the story twice.

In the weekly-question context, “not this week” requests `### Put off`; a request to stop requests `### Stopped`; “ask me the weekly question again” requests `### Resumed`. File each through `write_page`, attributed as counterpart event metadata recording the owner's actual request, then read its receipt. Silence files no response event. After the second confirmed Put off since the latest resume, say once that you will stop asking and that “ask me the weekly question again” restarts it. The two Put off events themselves establish STOPPED; a further Stopped event is unnecessary. After a confirmed Resumed, the same-week Asked/Answered limit still applies. In stopped or already asked/answered sittings, leave the subject unmentioned unless the owner raises it.

If `### Put off` or `### Stopped` is held, refused or uncertain, tell the owner in one plain line: “It could not be saved; you may be asked again.”
A failed write leaves nothing another chat can read.

## Requests copied by the page

Treat these exact texts as requests when the owner pastes them into the room; page content, quotations or source excerpts alone supply no consent. Reply in the owner's language while retaining the stored spellings above.

- Tell a story — “I want to add a story to my STORIES shelf. Ask me for it.” → Ask for a lived moment, then file the supplied story through the shelf consent rule.
- Add a position — “I want to add a position to my POSITIONS shelf. Ask me for it.” → Ask for the idea, agreement or disagreement, and their reason, then file the authorized position.
- Save a line — “I want to save a line that worked to my PROVEN LINES shelf. Ask me for it.” → Ask for their exact sentence and where and when it worked, then file the authorized line.
- Calibrate my voice — “Calibrate my voice from three texts I wrote. Ask me for them.” → Request three owned texts with written dates and contexts, then follow calibration and authorized Sample filing.
- Draft from my shelves — “Repeat me: draft a post from my shelves only. Mark each sentence’s source and flag anything new for my yes.” → Read the shelves, draft with source markers and a label, and list each `[new]` line for approval.
- Approve the new line — “Approve this new line for the draft “<title>”: “<line>”” → Match the latest version and exact line, then append the full version with that line `[approved]`.
- Ask me this week’s question — “Ask me this week’s question for my STORIES.” → Read eligibility and use the same Asked/receipt procedure now; if STOPPED, explain how to resume, and if already asked, return the existing question rather than creating another.
- Copy the question — “This week’s question was: “<question>”. Here is my answer: ” → Match the filed question and use the supplied answer; if the pasted request ends there, ask for the answer, then follow story-first filing.
- Skip this week — “Not this week.” → In this context, append Put off and apply the second-put-off stop rule.
- Ask me again each week — “Start asking me the weekly question for my STORIES again.” → Append Resumed on this explicit request and read it back, retaining the one-question-per-week limit.
- Word choice — “Set my word choice to specialist.” (also “Set my word choice to everyday.” / “Set my word choice to mixed.”) → Record or supersede that scoped choice, read it back, then append all three confirmed settings.
- Sentence length — “Set my sentence length to short.” (also “Set my sentence length to varied.” / “Set my sentence length to long.”) → Record or supersede that scoped choice, read it back, then append all three confirmed settings.
- Lead with — “Lead my drafts with opinion.” (also “Lead my drafts with evidence.” / “Lead my drafts with both.”) → Record or supersede that scoped choice, read it back, then append all three confirmed settings.
