# Thought artifact contracts

> Reference for writing-pack-idea-development. Read when the owner wants the outcome kept.

Create an artifact only when the owner asks to keep it or turn the conversation into something reusable. Choose the smallest format that preserves the insight. One canonical piece, revised in place during follow-up.

## Where it lives

- Always: the artifact in full in the chat.
- In the room: on their yes, a page in their library via `write_page` (`author: counterpart` — it is our synthesis, with their own lines quoted as theirs). Title it by format and subject, e.g. `Thesis — consistency compounds judgment`. Revisions are dated `append`s, never a second page.
- Terminal: the brain's notebooks, under `ideas-lab/thought-partner/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`, revised in place.
- A decision is theirs to state: if the owner states one in their own words, it belongs on the record under the room's filing rules; the memo stays a working document.

## Shared requirements

Include only sections that carry real signal, and omit any section that would be empty. The artifact fits one screen, roughly 350 to 500 words, unless the owner requested an outline. The section lists below are menus to draw from, not checklists, and supporting-evidence bullets cap at the three strongest. Identify brain sources by title when they materially shaped the result. Separate their direct evidence from the Thought Partner's synthesis.

Do not create duplicates for each revision. Update the relevant sections of the same piece, and tell them what changed.

## Idea map

Use when several related insights have emerged but no single thesis should dominate.

- Central question or territory
- Recurring patterns
- Productive contradictions
- Possible named concepts
- Supporting notes or examples
- Connections between ideas
- Open questions
- Promising directions to develop

## Thesis note

Use when the owner has a claim suitable for an essay, video, argument, strategy, or public point of view.

- One-sentence thesis
- What the thesis means
- Why it matters now
- Supporting evidence or experience
- Strongest opposing view
- Boundary: where the thesis does not apply
- Implications
- Questions to research or test

## Belief memo

Use for a personal or professional belief the owner wants to retain and revisit.

- What I currently believe
- What led me here
- Evidence and lived examples
- Values embedded in the belief
- What I may be missing
- What would change my mind
- How the belief changes my behavior

## Decision memo

Use when the conversation concerns a real choice.

- Decision to make
- Desired outcome
- Options considered
- Criteria and tradeoffs
- Key assumptions
- Reversible versus irreversible elements
- Current decision and confidence
- Next action
- Review trigger or date

## Principles note

Use when several cases reveal stable rules of thumb.

For each principle include the principle, the mechanism behind it, a concrete example, its boundary, and how to apply it. Avoid generic slogans. A principle should help them make a future decision.

## Content direction

Use when the insight is ready to become public work but the owner is not asking for a full draft.

- Core idea
- Audience tension
- Distinctive angle
- Evidence or story available
- Three possible formats or routes
- What must remain true in the final piece
- Recommended next artifact

## Outline

Use when the owner has selected a direction and wants its reasoning organized. Each section should advance the claim, introduce necessary evidence, answer an objection, or create an earned implication. Do not inflate the outline with decorative sections.
