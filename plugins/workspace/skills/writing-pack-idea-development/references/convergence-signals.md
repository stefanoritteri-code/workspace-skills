# Convergence signals

> Reference for writing-pack-idea-development. Read when deciding whether to keep exploring, synthesize, or stop.

Good thought partnership knows when to stop excavating. The objective is not the longest conversation; it is a meaningful increase in clarity that the owner can retain, test, or use.

## Maintain a working map

Track five elements throughout the conversation:

- **Current claim:** what appears true now.
- **Evidence:** examples, experience, the owner's material, or external facts supporting it.
- **Tension:** the contradiction, tradeoff, or opposing view that keeps the claim honest.
- **Open question:** the missing answer most likely to change the claim.
- **Implication:** what the owner might believe, decide, test, or create if the claim holds.

Do not display this structure after every turn. Use it internally to select the next question. Share a brief checkpoint when the conversation changes direction, accumulates several important findings, or risks circling.

## Keep exploring when

- the claim is still generic enough to fit anyone;
- the supporting example is missing or purely hypothetical;
- the main terms mean different things in different turns;
- an obvious counterexample would collapse the conclusion;
- the owner is describing symptoms but not the mechanism;
- the practical implication remains unclear;
- new material from the owner's notes materially changes the frame.

Ask the question that resolves the most important uncertainty, not one question for every gap.

## Offer synthesis when

- the claim is precise enough to disagree with;
- at least one concrete example or source supports it;
- the strongest tension or objection has been acknowledged;
- the claim has an honest boundary;
- a useful implication follows;
- the owner is repeating the same territory in new words.

State that the synthesis is a working interpretation, not settled truth. Use their language where accurate, but do not attribute your wording to them.

## Stop when

Additional questions would add decoration rather than change the map. Stop when the owner has reached a decision with understood tradeoffs, a thesis with support and boundaries, or an idea the owner is ready to test. Also stop when the owner requests an artifact. Do not withhold a useful output in pursuit of a fictional perfect breakthrough.

## Recovery from circling

Name the loop without blame. Summarize what is established, what remains unresolved, and the two plausible paths forward. Ask them to choose whether to gather evidence, make a provisional decision, change the frame, or pause. Circular conversation is often a sign that the remaining uncertainty cannot be solved by reflection alone.

## Calibrated ending

A good ending contains the clearest current formulation, the evidence or experience carrying it, the strongest qualification, and one next use. Offer the smallest suitable artifact. If the owner does not want to save anything, leave them with a compact statement the owner can return to rather than automatically creating clutter.
