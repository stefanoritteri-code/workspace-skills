# Shared invented case facts

Read once when preparing any confirmed seat. All quantities use invented, unspecified monetary units. These are comparison controls, never facts about an owner. The cards give provisional choices; each depth derives consequences and tests its own position against these same facts. No seat may quietly add favorable facts to win a comparison.

## Case 1: A large client asks for immediate availability

**Given facts.** A three-person studio can sell 80 delivery hours in a normal week after administration. Existing work uses 56. A prospective client offers 4,800 per month for an estimated 20 delivery hours each week, but also expects same-day changes and attendance at three weekly calls. The owner wants one uninterrupted afternoon for improving the service. The client has not yet accepted a response window or a limit on revisions. Figures are invented units for reasoning, not market prices.

**Live alternatives.** Accept the request as described; offer a paid pilot with explicit service boundaries; decline and keep capacity for existing work. A pilot is only a real option if the client accepts its boundaries before work starts.

**Do not assume.** The estimate may exclude call preparation, revisions and context recovery. Neither the probability of renewal nor the value of later referrals is established. No signed agreement is assumed.

**Evidence to retain.** Capture actual delivery time, call time, change requests, response expectations and contribution after direct costs separately. Keep evidence from existing clients too: harm displaced onto them would be easy to miss in the new account's numbers.

**A bounded first test.** A short paid pilot can test the offer only after both parties accept its scope. Reserve an exit conversation and do not describe a proposed pilot as already sold. Inspect the first week's interruptions before extrapolating a month.

**Other facts capable of reopening the choice.** A signed response window, measured lower coordination burden, loss of another account or an urgent cash need could change the choice. A prestigious logo by itself does not change the capacity calculation.

## Case 2: A service price has stayed still while effort increased

**Given facts.** A small practice sells a package for 900. Direct external costs are 180 and delivery usually takes 12 hours, before the owner's sales and administration. Six of the last ten suitable enquiries purchased. Recent requests have added a review round that was not in the original offer. The owner considers charging 1,200, keeping the old price with a narrower promise, or leaving the offer alone. No claim about the broader market follows from ten enquiries.

**Live alternatives.** Raise the price for a clearly described unchanged package; keep the price and remove a stated component; retain price and scope for a bounded comparison period. Existing accepted quotes need their own treatment and cannot silently change.

**Do not assume.** The actual variation in hours, reasons for four lost enquiries, buyer outcomes and cash timing are missing. A different mix of customers could explain a later change in conversion.

**Evidence to retain.** Record quote date, offered scope, source of enquiry, whether it was suitable, acceptance, actual hours, extras and customer outcome. Avoid comparing gross revenue with take-home income or pretending that an unpaid owner hour has no cost.

**A bounded first test.** Quote the revised offer to a small subsequent group of suitable prospects and inspect reasons, not just the count who buy. Explain any scope difference. Do not misrepresent a price experiment as a discount deadline or conceal changes from current customers.

**Other facts capable of reopening the choice.** A drop in suitable demand, a failure to deliver the advertised outcome, or evidence that the extra review prevents costly errors may overturn a simple increase or scope cut. One enthusiastic buyer is informative but not conclusive.

## Case 3: The owner wants a coordinator during a crowded period

**Given facts.** A four-person business reports roughly 15 owner hours a week spent on scheduling, chasing inputs and answering handover questions. It is considering a coordinator for 20 hours a week at a monthly cost of 1,600. Workload spiked after two projects began together. The task list contains repeated requests but no measured breakdown of avoidable versus necessary coordination. The team has not defined which decisions a new person could make without asking the owner.

**Live alternatives.** Hire into a defined recurring role; buy a bounded period of external help if permitted and useful; simplify intake and handovers before deciding. A hiring proposal does not authorize employment commitments or messages.

**Do not assume.** The persistence of demand, available cash, training effort, actual owner bottlenecks and candidate capability are unverified. The arithmetic cannot assume every hour delegated becomes useful owner time.

**Evidence to retain.** Track a representative work sample by task, recurrence, waiting cause, decision authority and recipient. Include the time colleagues spend teaching and correcting work. Seek consent for any observation; do not make individual surveillance the method.

**A bounded first test.** First test a written handover and a defined decision boundary on a small live workstream. If help is engaged, use an agreed role, support and a fair evaluation period. Do not promise a permanent role merely to discover whether there is work for it.

**Other facts capable of reopening the choice.** Stable recurring volume, a demonstrated capability gap or service failures that simplification cannot fix may justify hiring sooner. Conversely, the disappearance of the spike or an unresolved authority bottleneck weakens the case.

## Case 4: A useful offer is waiting behind an expanding launch list

**Given facts.** A two-person business has a usable service kit and eight people who agreed to hear when it is ready. Two early users tried one component and found it useful, but nobody has bought the complete offer. The launch list now includes a site redesign, an introductory video, a library of extras and automated delivery. Twelve workdays are available this month alongside existing promises. The owner hopes for repeat use rather than a single burst of attention.

**Live alternatives.** Offer a bounded paid version to the willing group; delay for a specified missing safety or quality requirement; build the larger launch. The smallest release must still honestly deliver the promised result.

**Do not assume.** Interest is not payment, use is not repeat use, and two component trials do not validate the whole offer. Delivery support, accessibility needs and buyer comprehension remain to be checked.

**Evidence to retain.** Preserve who was invited with consent, what was offered, what they paid or declined, where they became stuck, support time and what result they achieved. Record declined participation without converting silence into approval or rejection.

**A bounded first test.** Manually deliver one complete transaction where that is feasible. Observe how the recipient begins, finishes and asks for help. Improve a demonstrated obstacle before investing in a speculative extra. Set a limit on the team's time and cash for the trial.

**Other facts capable of reopening the choice.** A critical reliability gap, no suitable willing buyers, or support demands larger than the available interval may justify delay or redesign. A cosmetic preference is weaker evidence than failure to complete the promised task.

## Case 5: A commitment already made is consuming the margin

**Given facts.** A small consultancy promised twelve fortnightly workshops to a group. Four have happened and eight remain. Attendance has declined from ten people to six, preparation takes twice the planned time, and the owner would now prefer a different project. There is no evidence yet about why people stopped attending. The original promise, payment terms and participants' reliance need to be read before any change is proposed.

**Live alternatives.** Complete the original commitment; negotiate a mutually accepted smaller format; arrange a responsible handover or exit if the actual agreement permits it. Personal preference alone cannot settle what others are owed.

**Do not assume.** Participant outcomes, causes of absence, remaining preparation that can be reused, agreement terms and the cost of a fair remedy are unknown. Low attendance does not prove the work lacks value to those still relying on it.

**Evidence to retain.** Retain the actual promise, sessions delivered, preparation and support time, participant feedback and any accepted change. Separate dissatisfaction with the format from a failure to supply what was agreed.

**A bounded first test.** Ask the remaining participants about the value they need and test one permitted format improvement within the promise. Negotiate changes explicitly before acting on them. Count the cost of transition and remedy, not just the hours released.

**Other facts capable of reopening the choice.** Evidence of strong participant reliance, a solvable preparation problem or renewed owner commitment supports continuing. Evidence that the format cannot deliver its promised result strengthens renegotiation, with responsibility for the consequences.


## Disagreement records

These additional invented records hold facts constant. The linked cards contain each seat’s own recommendation once. Different provisional preferences remain subject to the owner’s ranking; no comparison invents a vote or a new fact.

### Record A

An existing client pays on time and produces a positive contribution after all recorded work. Its frequent corrections concern genuine defects. The owner dislikes the revisions, wants this kind of work overall, and has enough cash to replace the account gradually. A month of coached production could address the defects; orderly nonrenewal is also available.

### Record B

A useful monthly bulletin has no renewal promise. Its paying recipients report that the familiar editor is part of its value. The owner would rather stop editing, has a sufficient income floor, and could fund one properly supported handover edition to a willing replacement.

### Record C

A profitable bespoke report requires six repetitive hours per delivery. A tested reusable component would save four of them, costs twelve hours to finish, and has three committed uses with clear rights. The owner prefers to leave report work, but would consider a limited build before deciding. Upkeep is estimated, not yet observed.

### Record D

A studio sells an optional rush add-on. It is profitable, unwanted by the owner, and repeatedly causes rescheduling. No current rush orders remain. A brief review could establish whether one intake change would remove most disruption; discontinuation would still leave the business viable.

### Record E

A December commission is safe, profitable and fully resourced, including support. It fills all remaining work capacity. The owner wants the completed project, finds its middle stages tedious, and also values a self-directed study month. Neither choice breaks an obligation; no income emergency or fixed priority ranking settles the choice.

### Record F

A viable business can either stop an unpromised teaching offer or run a paid guest session. The owner dislikes the administration but reports a continuing wish to teach and is unsure whether that wish survives real classroom experience. The guest session needs preparation and has a supported host.

### Record G

A small subscription service is wanted by customers and viable after maintenance. The owner has sufficient alternatives, dislikes its routine, and has not promised renewal. A manager could operate it, but recruitment and training would consume a month of owner attention without a guaranteed handover.

### Record H

A new workshop can safely serve its first buyers. Feedback on practice sessions shows a weak but adequate explanation. Five willing prospective buyers are available now; a specialist can improve the explanation next month. Delaying sacrifices current market contact, while launching sacrifices a chance to improve first impressions.

### Record I

A firm can spend its spare capacity either learning a difficult technique that improves custom results or packaging an existing adequate technique for reuse. Both have plausible demand, but neither has committed buyers. The current team can maintain the package; only the owner can learn the advanced technique this month.

### Record J

A team shows more defects on heavily interrupted days, but those days also contain the hardest jobs. Next week it can either protect one complete production interval or spend the available improvement time classifying recent job difficulty. Neither is needed to meet a present emergency.

### Record K

A finite backlog could be cleared with a focused extra work session. It contains no overdue promises, and clearing it would make next week somewhat easier. The same session is the owner’s only chance this month to join a valued informal outing. Both choices are feasible; neither is a health or safety necessity.

### Record L

An owner’s work is financially sound but feels stale. They report both a technical plateau and curiosity about a different role. A focused improvement project and a short role-shadowing placement compete for the same available days. Neither route has been tried; existing work has coverage.

### Record M

A December commission would fill all remaining production capacity but has verified separate support coverage. Its standard version meets the buyer’s complete need. A more demanding version would develop valuable skill and improve quality, but would exceed December capacity. The buyer welcomes either, and no order has been accepted.

### Record N

A partner has willing buyers for a workshop on its platform. Terms permit the firm’s delivery but prohibit transferring participant contact records. The firm can also sell directly to a much smaller willing group. Both routes are viable; the partner controls future listing and discovery.

### Record O

Suitable prospects understand an offer but decline it. Existing records contain prices and outcomes, but no reasons. The owner can either speak with willing nonbuyers or first analyze which quoted scope predicts refusal. There is no immediate viability crisis.

### Record P

A creator made no publication promise but wants to launch a small paid series. Interested recipients say predictability would help them participate. The owner could sustain monthly delivery with a real loss of spontaneous free time, or offer occasional editions with no schedule. Both are financially viable.

### Record Q

A successful service is ready to relaunch. Recipients want it; delivery is feasible. The owner describes a persistent uncertainty about continuing this kind of role, and asks to examine it. A paid relaunch and an unpaid trial in another role compete for the same open period; no renewal is owed.

### Record R

A prospective 20% price increase would be viable and honest for a single standard service. Some willing buyers say they want private reassurance; others value visible participation with peers. Both groups obtain the same functional result. Two communication and participation routes are feasible, but would add coordination. No accepted quote changes.

### Record S

A repeated report can be generated automatically, but occasional omissions may come from either missing inputs or mistaken interpretation. The team has enough time for either a small reusable input check or an inspection of past omitted cases. Current delivery has manual review.

### Record T

A validated reusable product would repay its build effort through committed orders. Its maintenance is covered. Building now would use the owner’s only open week for a personally valued trip that cannot happen later this year. The business is viable without either choice, and the owner values both.

### Record U

An owner has a marketable archive with verified reuse rights and willing buyers. Packaging it would be financially useful but personally dull. They also report a wanted new practice with no demand evidence. Only one experiment fits; the business has a sufficient financial floor.

### Record V

A stable service has a small verified reusable component that could serve a second buyer group. A modest paid test fits spare capacity, but introduces separate updates and support. The current business is healthy; there is no need to diversify for survival.

### Record W

A team has two viable workflow options. A short comparison might identify a modest advantage, but would postpone a wanted project start. No irreversible exposure or missing obligation depends on the comparison. The owner wants to choose, yet also hopes to avoid any later regret.

### Record X

An owner reports repeatedly withdrawing from a profitable service despite adequate capacity. They recognize an unresolved direction question and invite reflection. Operational records also show that withdrawals cluster around one handover. A practical handover trial and a different-role experience compete for the next available period.

### Record Y

A recurring delivery exception has caused modest losses. Staff propose a one-page record to distinguish causes; the owner proposes removing the exception from future offers. Existing obligations can be completed either way, and the firm is viable without the exception. It is unclear whether the fault affects ordinary work too.

### Record Z

An owner finds a modest community contribution worthwhile but worries it does not express a larger direction. They invite reflection, yet have no recurring mismatch or unwanted duty to report. One open afternoon could be spent participating or examining longer-term options.

### Record AA

A viable shop could redesign its scheduling to reduce recurring inconvenience. The work fits a spare week, but that week is also the owner’s chosen time off. Current inconvenience is tolerable and does not break promises. No replacement operator can perform the redesign this month.

### Record AB

A founder can keep a calm profitable service at its present scale or try a supported larger role. They explicitly want to examine leadership, but have never occupied that role. Both choices preserve current obligations. The trial temporarily increases coordination and would not establish a permanent expansion.
