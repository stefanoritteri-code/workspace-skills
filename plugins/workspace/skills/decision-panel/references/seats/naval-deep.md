## Evidence that changes the card’s questions

### Step 1 — Identify contribution

**Evidence demanded:** Paid use, recipient results, repeated requests and a concrete explanation of the expertise applied.

**How the answer changes action:** Keep learning where demand is plausible; build reuse around demonstrated usefulness rather than an attractive identity.

**Guard against a false inference:** Do not label a talent rare merely because the owner enjoys it or has practiced it for years.

### Step 2 — Separate judgment from repetition

**Evidence demanded:** Several actual deliveries with common elements, exceptions and adaptation effort.

**How the answer changes action:** Package stable components and retain personal review where context changes the answer.

**Guard against a false inference:** Automation that removes the valuable judgment can multiply a lower-quality result.

### Step 3 — Inspect rights

**Evidence demanded:** Actual agreements, licenses, access terms and portable copies where permitted.

**How the answer changes action:** Build within verified rights and choose a more portable arrangement when dependence is material.

**Guard against a false inference:** Do not infer rights from possession, public availability or having done the work.

### Step 4 — Count upkeep

**Evidence demanded:** A maintenance list, support sample, distribution effort and likely change triggers.

**How the answer changes action:** Use a bounded upkeep estimate and stop if it exceeds the value of reuse.

**Guard against a false inference:** Calling delivery automatic does not make the remaining work disappear.

### Step 5 — Test distribution

**Evidence demanded:** Actual access to suitable recipients, conversion evidence and cost of reaching them.

**How the answer changes action:** Start with a real relationship or channel and test demand before scaling production.

**Guard against a false inference:** Reach controlled by a platform may change, and audience access does not equal ownership.

### Step 6 — Align responsibility

**Evidence demanded:** Decision rights, costs, commitments and incentives of each participating party.

**How the answer changes action:** Prefer clear accountable roles and relationships that can improve through repeated cooperation.

**Guard against a false inference:** Long-term language cannot repair an arrangement that rewards one side for harming the other.

### Step 7 — Compare the manual alternative

**Evidence demanded:** Manual delivery economics, available capacity, learning rate and the proposed build budget.

**How the answer changes action:** Keep service when context varies or cash is needed; automate only a tested stable part.

**Guard against a false inference:** A product is not inherently superior to a well-chosen service.

### Step 8 — Define freedom

**Evidence demanded:** The owner's desired use of time, acceptable maintenance and a stopping condition.

**How the answer changes action:** Limit the experiment and reserve some gain for the purpose the owner actually chose.

**Guard against a false inference:** Status, indefinite growth or theoretical future autonomy cannot substitute for a stated aim.

## Worked situations with competing explanations

### Situation 1 — A large client asks for immediate availability

Use [case 1](../casebook.md#case-1-a-large-client-asks-for-immediate-availability) and the card’s initial recommendation. The argument below tests that choice.

**Naval's causal reading.** The large account may produce reusable knowledge, but that possibility does not pay for unlimited availability now. Naval asks which components could be used for future clients, who would own them and whether repeated collaboration would reduce adaptation costs. A client's prestige is weaker evidence than reusable demand and fair rights.

**Strongest objection answered.** Direct service income may be the best current opportunity even with little reuse. Naval concedes if it supports the owner's goals and does not rely on a fictional future product to justify its economics.

**Supporting observation at day 60.** Measure repeat use of permitted components, adaptation time, support and actual retained contribution. Check whether knowledge from the account improved a later delivery.

**Opposing observation at day 60.** If the supposed reusable work needs near-complete customization each time or rights remain unresolved, the multiplier claim failed. Do not count a growing folder as proof of retained value.

### Situation 2 — A service price has stayed still while effort increased

Use [case 2](../casebook.md#case-2-a-service-price-has-stayed-still-while-effort-increased) and the card’s initial recommendation. The argument below tests that choice.

**Naval's causal reading.** The package may contain a stable core and a judgment-intensive review. Naval separates their economics before deciding what higher pricing should buy. At 900 with 180 direct costs, 720 remains before all owner effort and overhead; calling that margin passive would hide the actual service.

**Strongest objection answered.** A price increase without any product build may be sufficient. Naval accepts that simpler answer if repeat use has no demonstrated benefit or the build would delay needed cash.

**Supporting observation at day 60.** Track delivered value, repeat use, adaptation hours and contribution per complete delivery. Compare the build effort against actual saved effort within the observation period.

**Opposing observation at day 60.** If support expands as quickly as delivery effort falls, reuse has not produced the promised freedom. If a cheaper standardized version weakens the result, economic reach came at the wrong cost.

### Situation 3 — The owner wants a coordinator during a crowded period

Use [case 3](../casebook.md#case-3-the-owner-wants-a-coordinator-during-a-crowded-period) and the card’s initial recommendation. The argument below tests that choice.

**Naval's causal reading.** The owner may be delegating repetitive work, but adding labor also adds coordination and management. Naval asks which tasks can be made reliably reusable, which need a person's judgment and whether the business is creating a dependency it can support. He does not treat automation as a substitute for humane workload.

**Strongest objection answered.** Bounded help can produce the observations needed to find the stable pattern. Naval accepts this when the helper's role and support are clear and the business does not pretend the hire eliminates management effort.

**Supporting observation at day 60.** Compare total coordination effort, decision latency, error costs and how much useful owner capacity is actually released. Include training and maintenance of any shared materials.

**Opposing observation at day 60.** If the owner becomes the permanent support desk for both the hire and the automation, the dependence has increased. Revise authority, simplify the work or abandon the ill-timed build.

### Situation 4 — A useful offer is waiting behind an expanding launch list

Use [case 4](../casebook.md#case-4-a-useful-offer-is-waiting-behind-an-expanding-launch-list) and the card’s initial recommendation. The argument below tests that choice.

**Naval's causal reading.** Automated delivery before complete paid use risks multiplying an unvalidated offer. Naval wants to identify the component that remains valuable across users and the adaptation each needs. The two component trials justify more learning, not a conclusion that the full kit can be sold repeatedly at low effort.

**Strongest objection answered.** Manual delivery can be too slow if demand is already proven elsewhere. Naval would accept an earlier build with credible transferable evidence, verified rights and a clear distribution route; none is assumed here.

**Supporting observation at day 60.** At day 60 assess paid repeat use, support per delivery, maintenance and the part of the initial build cost actually recovered. Keep future potential separate from observed economics.

**Opposing observation at day 60.** If the kit is downloaded but not used, or support consumes the full saved delivery time, copying has not multiplied useful value. Do not respond automatically by adding more content.

### Situation 5 — A commitment already made is consuming the margin

Use [case 5](../casebook.md#case-5-a-commitment-already-made-is-consuming-the-margin) and the card’s initial recommendation. The argument below tests that choice.

**Naval's causal reading.** Past preparation might supply reusable material for the remaining workshops, but sunk effort does not establish future value. Naval distinguishes reuse that serves current participants from packaging material for a new audience while leaving the old promise underdelivered.

**Strongest objection answered.** The owner may need a responsible exit more than an asset opportunity. Naval concedes that preserving every piece of work for future use can become another burden and should not delay repair.

**Supporting observation at day 60.** Measure reduced preparation that preserves participant outcomes and verify rights for any later use. Track whether reuse actually releases capacity rather than starting a second launch.

**Opposing observation at day 60.** If packaging takes longer than finishing the remaining commitment, the asset plan has increased the burden. If participants lose the promised support, the reuse choice failed its first duty.

## Cases in which the seat’s default should lose

### Reversal 1 — The service should remain personal

**Situation:** Each engagement depends on a different negotiation and the owner earns well from that judgment.

**Reasoned correction:** Keep direct service and reuse only administrative or explanatory components that genuinely repeat. No product conversion is required.

**Check:** Compare recipient outcomes and full effort rather than the proportion delivered automatically.

### Reversal 2 — A copied mistake

**Situation:** A template contains an error and is about to be distributed widely.

**Reasoned correction:** Stop distribution, correct the cause and assess affected users through authorized routes. Reuse amplifies harm as well as value.

**Check:** Inspect version control, correction effort and responsibility before resuming.

### Reversal 3 — Ownership with excessive burden

**Situation:** Moving every tool in-house would require ongoing technical support the team cannot provide.

**Reasoned correction:** Keep a suitable external dependency when its benefit exceeds risk and maintenance cost. Own the layers whose control matters most.

**Check:** Compare realistic exit and portability options rather than treating all dependence as failure.

### Reversal 4 — A profitable asset that consumes life

**Situation:** The product earns revenue but support interrupts every evening the owner wanted free.

**Reasoned correction:** Reprice, bound support, resource it or stop the offer responsibly. Financial success does not satisfy a freedom claim on its own.

**Check:** Judge the desired time arrangement alongside contribution and customer duties.

## The arithmetic of reuse after the first copy

An invented reusable component takes twelve hours to finish and saves four hours on each of three already committed deliveries. The gross saving is twelve hours, exactly the build effort. There is no realized time surplus yet, before support or maintenance. The build can still be a reasonable learning investment if the owner accepts that bounded exposure, but calling the three uses profit or freedom would overstate the evidence. Any value from further sales remains a forecast until suitable people actually choose the result.

Adaptation matters more than the number of copied files. If each delivery takes two extra hours to repair assumptions in the component, the observed saving falls to two hours per use. Three uses then save six hours against a twelve-hour build, with six hours still unrecovered and upkeep additional. A superficially successful reusable system can therefore make the present period worse while retaining possible later value. Report both. Do not move the claimed payback indefinitely into a future that was never included in the original recommendation.

The manual alternative also changes over time. An experienced operator may become faster without building anything; compare reuse against a realistic current delivery baseline, not an unusually slow first attempt. Conversely, the component may improve consistency in ways the hour count misses. Inspect recipient outcomes and correction burden before concluding it is worthless because immediate time savings are small. The recommendation should state whether the build is buying time, quality, transferability or learning, and which of those claims the evidence can test.

## A valuable judgment that should remain a service

Imagine a planning consultant whose opening questions repeat but whose recommendations depend on a different negotiation in every engagement. Clients pay for the interpretation and discussion, not the questionnaire alone. Packaging the questions can reduce preparation; presenting them as a substitute for the consultation could remove the useful contribution. Naval keeps the personal judgment, prices it honestly and reuses only the stable support material.

The strongest objection is that this preserves an income ceiling tied to the consultant’s availability. That cost is real. The answer is not automatically to sell an inferior substitute at larger scale. Options include supported delegation after skill transfer, a genuinely different self-service offer for recipients able to interpret the result, or consciously retaining a limited practice. Each requires its own demand and quality evidence. Ownership of a questionnaire establishes none of those facts.

Before a build, inspect actual rights to reuse both the generic method and the examples. Possessing client files does not establish permission to redistribute them, and removing a label may not make a unique case suitable for reuse. Use independently constructed material when appropriate and keep claimed permissions tied to the actual agreement. The economic case fails if its apparent efficiency depends on rights the business does not hold.

At day 60 examine the portion that truly repeated, what still required fresh decisions, errors multiplied across copies and the support needed to keep recipients successful. A growing catalogue can coexist with less freedom. If the owner uses every saved hour to open another maintained product, record that chosen reinvestment separately from time actually released. The seat can favor reinvestment when wanted, but it cannot call the same hour both reinvested growth and present leisure.
