# Personal brand strategy contract

> Reference for writing-pack-personal-brand. Read when delivering, saving, or revising the strategy.

ONE strategy, titled `Personal Brand Strategy: <name or brand>`. It is the canonical strategy for the conversation. Do not repeat the title as an H1 inside the body. Do not create separate documents for every section unless supporting pieces are explicitly requested.

## Where it lives

- Always: the full strategy in the chat.
- In the room: on the owner's yes, a page in their library via `write_page` (`author: counterpart` — our synthesis, with their own lines quoted as theirs). Refinements are dated `append`s restating the affected sections in full, never a second page.
- Terminal: the brain's notebooks, under `ideas-lab/personal-brand/<brand-slug>/strategy.md`, edited in place.

## Order

### Brand thesis and lens

A compact statement of the recognizable audience, the change they want, and the person's distinct interpretation. State the anti-vision and vision poles concretely, then add one sentence that tests every future content idea against the movement from A to B. Mark it as a working thesis when important assumptions remain.

### Smallest viable audience

Paint the person through situation, tension, prior attempts, unspoken desire, and desired capability. Avoid a demographic profile unless demographics materially affect the work.

### Personal monopoly and credibility

Explain the change served, the combination that makes the work distinct, and the relevant path that earns trust: the before, the turning points, the earned lessons. Separate lived experience, practiced skill, observation, and study. Name proof gaps honestly.

### Beliefs, tensions, and associations

List the durable ideas to repeat, defaults to challenge, extreme-but-defensible points of view, desired associations to build, and non-associations that create boundaries.

### Topic tree

Roughly three broad branches. Open each branch with one line stating how it moves the audience from A toward B ("this branch moves them from A toward B because..."); reframe or drop a branch that cannot earn that line. Under each, at most six to eight one-line entries total across topics, questions, stories, arguments, and talking points. The tree is a starting inventory, not an exhaustive catalog. Keep branches connected to genuine expertise and interest.

### What to write about

A permission menu so the person actually starts. Frame content as building a library of personal knowledge; it does not matter how basic a piece feels, the library is the point. List the post types that always work: extreme beliefs, ideas they wish they had written, problems people face, arguments with content they read, opinions on popular topics, interesting facts about the topic, their take on top-performing content, lessons from their own journey, advice to their past, present, or future self, harsh truths about achieving a goal, and aspirational advice that tells people what to do and why. Anything qualifies as long as it answers "what's in it for me?". The through-line: just pick one and write the post.

### Initial post directions

At most ten one-liners. Each carries a claim or question, audience relevance, and proof source. Mix beliefs, stories, mistakes, frameworks, observations, and advice to a past self.

### First-month activation

Recommend a sustainable cadence, a balanced set of initial pieces, and a simple evidence review. Favor learning and consistency over vanity targets.

### Inviting people into your worldview

Zoom out to the three to six month picture. Content over time creates a world. Across everything published, the person should consistently get across the enemy (the anti-vision), the ideal life (the vision), the beliefs people need to adopt, and the skills people need to learn. This stays the standing lens on all future content.

## Budget

A strategy may run long, but it may never repeat itself. No paragraph over two lines. Keep the whole strategy readable in one sitting, roughly 1,000 words; that number is a ceiling, never a floor. Omit any section with nothing real to say.

Write in clear Markdown with short paragraphs and useful bullets. Remove decorative blank lines and duplicate headings. Every correction or refinement updates this same strategy rather than creating a competing version.
