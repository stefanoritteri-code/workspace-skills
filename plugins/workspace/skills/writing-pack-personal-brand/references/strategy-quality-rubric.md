# Personal brand strategy quality rubric

> Reference for writing-pack-personal-brand. Use silently before presenting or updating the strategy.

A strong strategy should score well across coherence, specificity, credibility, distinctiveness, sustainability, and activation.

## Coherence

The audience, anti-vision, vision, mission, personal monopoly, topic tree, and content directions should describe one world. Each topic branch must connect to the transformation. If the strategy could be rearranged without changing its meaning, it is probably a collection of generic sections rather than a coherent point of view.

## Specificity

The audience should recognize themselves in situations and tensions, not demographic labels. The anti-vision and vision should change real behaviors, decisions, or capabilities. Post directions should contain a claim or question rather than broad labels such as mindset, productivity, or business.

## Credibility

Every strong claim should have an honest basis in lived experience, practiced skill, observation, study, or external evidence. Never invent proof. Distinguish a personal belief from a general fact. Name what still needs to be demonstrated.

## Distinctiveness

The personal monopoly must change the work, not merely the tagline. Look for a combination of experiences, interests, methods, and beliefs that affects examples, language, audience understanding, and editorial choices. Desired associations and non-associations should give the brand recognizable edges.

## Sustainability

The topic tree should support ongoing learning and creation without trapping the person in a tiny niche. The cadence and formats should fit their energy, skills, and real schedule. A strategy that requires an imaginary version of the person will not survive.

## Activation

The strategy must make the next useful action obvious. Initial post directions should be strong enough to hand straight to a writing skill. The first-month plan should create evidence without becoming a rigid thirty-day content factory.

Reject common failure modes: copied creator voice, inflated origin stories, generic audience avatars, arbitrary pillars, vague aspirations, monetization plans nobody requested, competitor research used as identity, and huge calendars full of interchangeable ideas. Revise until the strategy sounds like this person and helps them choose what to publish next.
