# Topic tree and content map

> Reference for writing-pack-personal-brand. Read when building the topic tree and post directions.

The topic tree turns broad interests into a durable publishing engine. Start with roughly three interest branches that genuinely belong to the person's life and expertise. Each branch should be broad enough to support years of learning and specific enough to produce recognizable questions, examples, and points of view.

For each branch, develop:

- core topics the audience needs to understand;
- subtopics and recurring questions;
- problems, mistakes, and false assumptions;
- skills, practices, and decision frameworks;
- personal stories and turning points;
- observations from current work;
- arguments with common advice;
- examples, demonstrations, and useful artifacts.

These categories are for choosing entries, not for filling out. The saved tree lists only the strongest six to eight entries per branch; everything else stays in reserve for future publishing.

The tree is an inventory, not the final editorial strategy. The content map connects every branch to the audience transformation. Place the anti-vision on one side and the vision on the other. Explain how each topic helps the audience diagnose the current state, change a belief, develop a capability, choose a path, or sustain the better state.

Remove or reframe a topic when it cannot ladder to the transformation. Do not delete a genuine interest merely because the connection is not obvious. Look for the person's distinctive interpretation. A designer interested in psychology and business may connect all three through helping independent creators make clearer decisions. The intersection is more useful than forcing one interest to win.

Generate post directions from the tree by combining a topic with an editorial job. Useful jobs include teaching a mechanism, naming a problem, challenging an assumption, telling a relevant story, showing work, comparing choices, explaining a mistake, sharing an observation, giving advice to a past self, or making a defensible prediction.

Each direction should include a claim or question, audience relevance, and source of credibility. "Write about productivity" is not a direction. "Why elaborate productivity systems fail creators who have not chosen a meaningful project" has tension, audience, and a point of view.

Treat the tree as editable. Publishing will reveal which branches create recognition, which require stronger proof, and which feel alive for the person. Update the structure from evidence without abandoning the central worldview every week.
