# The value equation

> Reference for writing-pack-offer-design. Read when auditing an offer or writing the Value Equation Analysis.

The core diagnostic for any offer: **perceived value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice)**. The two numerator levers raise what the prospect believes they will get; the two denominator levers lower what it costs them in time and pain to get it. Price is not in the equation — an offer that maximizes the equation supports a premium price; an offer that ignores it has to discount.

## The four levers

1. **Dream outcome** — the state the prospect actually wants, not the product's function. Nobody wants a course; they want the retainer income, the audience, the body. Articulate the outcome in the prospect's own words, and size it honestly: the outcome must be one THIS product can credibly produce.
2. **Perceived likelihood of achievement** — the prospect's belief that it will work FOR THEM. Raised by proof (results from people like them), by a named mechanism that explains why this works when other things failed, by specificity, and by risk reversal. This lever is where most weak offers die: a big promise with no reason to believe subtracts value.
3. **Time delay** — how long until the first meaningful result. Shrink it structurally, not rhetorically: a fast-win component in week one, a done-for-you head start, a template that produces something on day one. Then name the honest timeframe. "Results in 30 days" only helps if 30 days is true.
4. **Effort and sacrifice** — what the prospect must give up or push through. Reduce it by changing the delivery mechanism (done-with-you beats do-it-yourself), removing steps, or absorbing the hard parts. What cannot be removed should be reframed honestly as the price of the outcome — hiding required effort produces refunds, not sales.

## Auditing an offer

Walk the four levers in order and score each: is the dream outcome stated as the prospect's outcome or as the product's features? What is the single strongest reason to believe, and is it shown or claimed? What is the honest time to first result, and does anything in the offer shorten it? What effort does the offer quietly demand? The weakest lever is the one to rebuild first — improving an already-strong lever is polish, fixing the weakest is leverage.

## Rules

- Never inflate a lever with a claim the owner cannot substantiate; an unbelievable promise LOWERS perceived likelihood and therefore total value.
- The levers interact: shrinking time delay (a fast win) is often the cheapest way to raise perceived likelihood.
- State in the blueprint's Value Equation Analysis which levers the offer pulls hardest and which remain weak — an honest weak spot the owner knows about beats a padded analysis.
