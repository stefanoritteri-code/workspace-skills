# The diagnostic interview

> Reference for writing-pack-offer-design. Read when something material is missing and you have to ask.

The interview exists to feed the blueprint, not to be thorough. Its rules keep it short, adaptive, and grounded in what already exists.

## Before the first question

Check the conversation and the owner's record first. A customer avatar answers the audience questions; a positioning doc answers differentiation; an existing sales page reveals the current promise, mechanism, and guarantee; a client dossier answers most of it for a client's offer. Read what exists and open by confirming, not re-asking: "Your avatar says X — still true?" costs one line and builds trust; re-interviewing material the owner already wrote burns their patience. If nothing exists, one compact opener: who it is for, and what the product or service is — plus an invitation to paste or link anything the owner has.

## The question discipline

- ONE question per message. Build each question on the previous answer; never run a fixed questionnaire.
- Skip any question a reference or earlier answer already covers — the fastest interview is the best one.
- Prefer questions that produce facts over questions that produce opinions: "what happened with the last three customers" beats "what makes you different."
- When an answer is vague, push once for the number: outcomes need magnitude and timeframe ("how much, by when, for how many of them"). If the owner does not have the number, note it as a proof gap rather than inventing one.

## Coverage checklist (take only what is missing)

1. Audience: who exactly, what situation, what they have already tried.
2. Awareness level: what the audience already believes about the problem and the solution category.
3. Delivery mechanism today, and appetite for changing it.
4. The problems the product solves best, in the customer's words.
5. Outcomes with numbers and timeframes; the best real result and the typical one.
6. Competitive landscape: what alternatives the prospect weighs, including "do nothing."
7. Guarantee today, refund tolerance, and any results the owner can prove.

## Ending the interview

Build as soon as the blueprint's components are supported — or the moment the owner signals readiness. Fill remaining gaps with industry-informed inference, and mark every inference in the blueprint as a suggestion, distinct from what the owner verified. A blueprint with three labeled suggestions ships and gets corrected; an interview that runs to completeness dies in chat.
