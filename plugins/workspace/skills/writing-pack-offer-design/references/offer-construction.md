# Constructing the offer stack

> Reference for writing-pack-offer-design. Read when building the stack and naming the mechanism.

The construction method that turns one product into an offer with layered, articulated value. Work it as a sequence; each step feeds the next.

## Step 1 — list every problem

Take the dream outcome and list every problem the prospect hits on the way to it — before buying, during, and after. Include the obstacles the product does not currently address: "I don't have time," "I've tried before and failed," "I won't know if it's working," "my situation is different." Be exhaustive here; this list is the raw material for everything else.

## Step 2 — turn each problem into a solution

For each problem, write the solution as an outcome statement: "how to get X without Y." At this stage ignore feasibility and cost — the point is a complete map of what a perfect offer would solve.

## Step 3 — choose delivery vehicles

For each solution, decide HOW it gets delivered, and grade the options against two axes: value to the prospect and cost to the owner. One-on-one is high value, high cost; group is medium/low; recorded or templated is lower value but near-zero marginal cost. The craft is finding high-value, low-cost vehicles: a template that saves ten hours, a checklist that removes a failure mode, a small group critique that feels personal. The delivery mechanism question from the interview lives here — often the single biggest value jump is changing the format (do-it-yourself → done-with-you → done-for-you), not adding content.

## Step 4 — trim and stack

Cut every solution that is low value regardless of cost, and every one that is high cost without proportionate value. What survives is the stack: the core mechanism plus the components that remove the prospect's stated obstacles. Each stack item earns its place by killing a named objection or shortening a value-equation lever. A stack item nobody's objection asked for is padding, and padding erodes believability.

## Naming the mechanism

The stack's core method deserves a name — a proprietary label for the way this offer produces the outcome ("the X System," "the Y Method"). A named mechanism raises perceived likelihood ("there is a system, not just effort"), differentiates against competitors selling the same category, and gives the landing page its transformation story. Name it descriptively enough to suggest the outcome, distinctively enough to be ownable. Lay it out in three to six steps in the blueprint; each step should be a real phase of the delivery, not a renamed table of contents.

## Rules

- Features and benefits in the blueprint come from this stack: every feature is paired with the obstacle it removes or the lever it pulls — never listed bare.
- The stack serves the ONE dream outcome. A second outcome is a second offer; recommend splitting rather than diluting.
