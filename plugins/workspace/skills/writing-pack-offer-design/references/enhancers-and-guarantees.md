# Enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees

> Reference for writing-pack-offer-design. Read when designing scarcity, urgency, bonuses or the guarantee.

Enhancers raise perceived value and prompt action without touching the core stack. Every enhancer has an honest version and a manipulative version; only the honest version is allowed, because a caught lie destroys perceived likelihood — the offer's most fragile lever.

## Scarcity (limited supply)

Real capacity limits only: cohort seats, client slots, physical units. State the limit and the reason ("I personally review every submission, so 20 seats"). Fake counters and evergreen "only 3 left" are forbidden — recommend removing them from existing offers, and say why: the audience notices, and the offer's claims stop being believed wholesale.

## Urgency (limited time)

Real deadlines only: an actual cohort start date, an actual price change, a bonus that genuinely expires because it is tied to a date. Rolling honest urgency is legitimate (each cohort really does close); manufactured countdowns are not. If the offer has no natural deadline, it is usually better to create a real one (a start date) than to fake one.

## Bonuses

A bonus is a stack item framed as an addition, and it obeys stack rules: each bonus kills a named objection or shortens time-to-result. Present each with its own name, its own value articulation, and the obstacle it removes. Three sharp bonuses beat ten fillers — a pile of vague bonuses reads as garage-sale clutter and lowers believability. Where possible, a bonus should be high value to the buyer and low marginal cost to the seller (templates, swipe files, recorded workshops, a diagnostic call).

## Guarantees and risk reversal

The guarantee moves risk from buyer to seller, and its creativity matters more than its size:

- **Unconditional** — money back, no questions. Strongest signal, simplest, right default for low-touch products.
- **Conditional** — "do the work, and if no result, refund" with the conditions listed plainly. Right for high-touch offers where effort is genuinely required; the conditions must be achievable and checkable, or it reads as a trap.
- **Outcome-based** — tied to the quantified outcome ("book 3 clients in 60 days or we work with you free until you do"). The strongest form when it can honestly be backed; it converts the Quantifiable Outcome into proof.
- **Anti-guarantee** — "all sales final, because X." Legitimate only where consumption is instant (information, access) AND the rest of the offer carries the believability load; use sparingly.

Design the guarantee against the prospect's actual feared risk (wasting money, wasting time, looking foolish), not generically. A creative reversal that names the fear beats a bigger number that doesn't. Never propose a guarantee the owner's economics cannot survive — ask about refund tolerance before recommending an aggressive one.

## Rules

- Every enhancer must survive the question "is this literally true?" If it cannot, cut it.
- Enhancers are presented in the blueprint under Risk Reversal and Features & Benefits — not as a separate hype section.
