# Visual controls and existing work

Infer audience, surface, desired effect and evidence of taste before proposing a visual system. State a one-line reading in ordinary words. Select what success means on that surface: persuade someone to decide; help someone operate a task; support reading/understanding; or let someone experience the work. These are design purposes, not four mandatory workflows.

Three optional conversational controls make corrections concrete:

| Control | Lower end | Higher end | Inspect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Composition | Orderly, predictable grid | Surprising, asymmetric arrangement | Reading order, orientation and purposeful contrast |
| Movement | Still or restrained | Stronger motion and transitions | What the movement communicates; readability and reduced-motion alternative |
| Density | More space and fewer items | More information at once | Scanability, priority and actual task needs |

Infer from the brief and accepted examples; the owner can say “quieter”, “more space”, or “more surprising” without choosing numbers. No universal 8/6/4 baseline, forced movement, palette, font or aesthetic. Distinguish typography, spacing, color, motion and structure so a correction changes the relevant property rather than everything.

Audit existing identity, screenshots/actual output, assets, content hierarchy, interactions, routes and constraints. Missing documentation alone does not mean a blank slate. **Preserve** retains identity and agreed behavior while improving scoped weaknesses. **Overhaul** replaces visual language when requested while preserving truth, content and agreed functionality. Resolve that choice only if unclear; do not quietly turn polish into a redesign or dilute an authorized overhaul into small tweaks.

For branded work inventory real logo/wordmark, product photos, interface screenshots, colors, fonts and guidelines; use only those relevant. Note actual version, provenance, resolution and missing inputs. Logo/photos/screenshots matter, not only colors/fonts. Use supplied files or an identified permitted connector; no imaginary download, official package or licensed asset. Do not invent testimonials, brands, dates or data to look authentic.

Use a small representative preview; two or three distinct directions help when the brief needs a choice, but one is enough for a clear brief. Preserve routes, tracking-dependent names, factual/legal copy and accessibility gains unless the scoped change genuinely authorizes changing them. Design-system and platform claims need actual current source material, not assumptions inherited from downloaded examples.

Before saying verified, inspect the real result at relevant sizes, including reduced motion when movement exists. A written browser test is not a performed test. Stop when the brief and checks are satisfied; remaining material defects stay disclosed rather than hidden by a polishing limit.
