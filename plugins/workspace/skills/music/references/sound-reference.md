# Decode a sound reference

Identify exact reference/version, period, scene and requested element: bass, drums, vocal treatment, harmony, space, rhythm or structure. A name can span incompatible sounds. If interpretation changes the work, ask one focused question with concrete alternatives; otherwise state the chosen reading. For a long piece establish the interval. Keep exact source/version in permitted research provenance; use neutral “Reference A” in operational output.

Read existing analyses/corrections and actual sources, distinguishing notes, creator statements, credits and listening observations. Secondary descriptions can guide inquiry but do not prove equipment or recording chain. For uncertain or recent claims use actual permitted primary-source access; without a public-source connector ask the owner to open and upload the relevant source with URL/date. No invented URLs, quotes or assertions that information does not exist.

A link, title or transcript is not listening. The door offers no audio-analysis tool. Only use a connector's audio operation if actually exposed, verified and allowed; otherwise the owner listens/measures in their audio software or prepared local analysis and returns version, interval, method and output as readable text/table/image. Attribute listening to its performer and measurements to the named software; without evidence leave sonic verification open.

Separate heard/measured (performer, method, material, interval), source-declared (dated citation), owner-described, and interpretive hypothesis with a disconfirming test. Do not claim exact synth/effect/microphone, BPM, key, compression ratio or frequency from impression. Estimated ranges remain estimates; half/double tempo can describe the same pulse.

Decode relevant functions: instrument roles; attacks/durations; subdivisions, accents, anticipations and rests; bass/drum relationship; register/density; timbre; space/depth; stereo width; vocal treatment; section development. Explain a concrete mechanism—short notes leave attack space, an offbeat response shifts emphasis, a long tail fills silence—as a testable interpretation when not documented. Replace “warm”, “epic” or “vintage” with audible properties.

Deliver a reference card: neutral label/version/interval and chosen element; anatomy at evidenced precision; concise standalone production language without artist/person/group/album/song/studio names, aliases or lyric quotations; specific unwanted drifts; evidence/confidence per claim; one small original trial with an audible criterion and a question checking the owner's intent. Exclusions become listening criteria if the destination tool lacks such a field.

Invented operational example: monophonic synth bass with short attacks and syncopated answers between kick hits, dry drums and few cymbals, sparse middle-register chords, short room and close vocal. This is a proposed description, not analysis of a piece actually heard. Show the card before it guides production; do not reconfirm an already clear interpretation.

The producer controls the sound. Deliver actual input requirements only from current provided/accessible tool documentation; do not promise generation or platform acceptance. File via [landing.md](landing.md): Sound research page with sources separate from name-free production text, versions and limits; exact chosen direction with `page`; agreed deadline trial task. At return compare the intended element and result. A good prompt proves no sonic match, and similar timbre proves no hypothesized recording technique.
