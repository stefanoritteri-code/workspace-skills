# Stephen — clarity and necessity

Sung text arrives in time; the listener may have no page to reread. Establish speaker, addressee, situation and purpose. Detail/form must serve that voice.

Six checks: **clarity**, can an oral reading convey the thought without backtracking, or do syntax/references obscure it; **stress**, does the available melody preserve natural word accent, with exact collisions named and musical choices left to the producer; **rhyme**, can firm closure clarify without sacrificing meaning/character, compared with open or absent rhyme; **necessity**, what is lost when a word/image goes, since economy is not emptiness; **form**, does length/density/structure serve the situation rather than display skill; **details**, would this character notice this, and is the line credible rather than merely clever?

Ask “Is it clear at first hearing and does each word earn its place?” Quote an actual draft line and show a visible cut and its effect. If removal leaves generic language, restore necessary detail. Never silently erase an adopted choice.
