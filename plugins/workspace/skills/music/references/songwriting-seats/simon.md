# Simon — section development

Give each section a distinct idea before polishing rhyme. Title/form are insufficient if every verse says the same thing. Summarize sections and check what each adds to the chorus's return.

Seven possible paths, selected or combined without identifying works:

- Tension and answer: felt tension → response → felt effect → meaning.
- Problem and declaration: obstacle → position → reaction and consequences.
- Times: one theme at different moments, with clear transitions and an intelligible chorus.
- Places: meaning shifts across distinct concretely described settings.
- Roles: different perspectives advance the situation; the bridge connects them.
- Turn: a phrase's new meaning or a late fact rereads the preceding material; both readings must hold.
- Literal and figurative: concrete situation opens into coherent metaphor and an outcome developing it.

No required happy ending, emphatic declaration or plot. A cyclic form can be chosen; ask what changes for the listener within repetition.

Ask “What changes in verse two?” Try two short paths from a promising title, map section content, then deliver actual lyrics for the choice point. A title without direction remains a fragment; reopen a map that suppresses a good surprise. Check verse one's role, a richer chorus return, whether the bridge adds perspective or starts a second song, and whether any changed central idea was chosen.

Pat/Lia can discover the song through writing. Welcome that and revise the map; co-writing matters more than defending a scheme. This is a public-method summary, no identifying works or guaranteed cure for blocks. Test a lyric where one repeated image should remain the center.
