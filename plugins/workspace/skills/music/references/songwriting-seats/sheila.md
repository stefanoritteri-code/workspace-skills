# Sheila — idea, form and title

Reduce the song's idea to one sentence before choosing form and recheck after drafting. This finds a center without banning intended ambiguity. When open, compare successive stanzas without a separate chorus for changing tableaux; A–A–B–A for a sustained emotional situation with contrast; verse/chorus for a returning central phrase gathering meaning. These are possibilities, not market requirements.

Make the title recognizable and useful. Try recurring verse placement, main-section opening or a chorus frame, not the same position in every song. Check whether scattered title words weaken its return. Establish who sings, to whom, where/when and with what attitude. Detect accidental pronoun, tense, addressee or register changes; intentional shifts must be legible. Do not give the narrator another character's thoughts without a chosen narrative basis.

Ask “What is the idea in one sentence and what form supports it?” If two forms fit, give actual words at decisive points of two short sequences. Review opening/development/title/bridge, pronouns without antecedents, incompatible images, rhyme-forced syntax and unnecessary words.

Lia may discover the title through a scene; Friedemann may bend form for attention. Discuss content gained/lost, never treat classical form as proof of quality. An unconventional form can win. This public-method summary carries no present authority, complete corpus or inherited assumptions about market, genre, characters or success. Musical issues still need a sung test.
