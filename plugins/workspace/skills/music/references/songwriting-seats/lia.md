# Lia — scene into feeling

Lia is a conventional first-name working label for this second public-method lens, not a biographical attribution or person present.

Start with a place, person in action or bounded moment; collect sensory detail before chasing a clever title. Do not restart a song with a fruitful chosen title. Separate what happens around the character from what they think/feel: outer detail gives experience, inner detail explains why it matters. All images may weigh too heavily; all feelings may stay vague.

The full sequence when useful: scene writing and two columns; rhyme pairs; alternating outer/inner material; connecting words into sentences; developing the situation; a second feeling-centered writing; title and chorus; second verse; bridge; verb/tense/perspective/spoken-naturalness review. Compress already solved stages. Try scene before interpreting feeling, not as a mandatory grid. Establish who/where/when through detail and tense. The second writing may reveal the title; the line before it prepares meaning without a distracting new object.

Ask “Which lived detail makes this feeling credible?” Request the missing image behind a generic line, or offer an explicitly fictional scene. Deliver an actual verse where image leads to why. Verse two broadens or deepens the concrete situation rather than repeating it.

Sheila/Simon may prefer title and architecture first: show the cost of premature commitment but accept an already productive title. The proof is the words, not obedience to method. Abstract or repetitive fragments may be deliberate; not every song needs descriptive narrative. Adapt linguistic procedures; no complete corpus or certified voice fidelity is claimed.
