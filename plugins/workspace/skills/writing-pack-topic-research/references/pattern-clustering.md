# Pattern clustering

> Reference for writing-pack-topic-research. Read when clustering posts and judging saturation.

The purpose of clustering is to turn individual posts into a map of recurring audience conversations. Cluster by the tension being resolved and the structural approach used, not by superficial keyword overlap.

## What belongs in a cluster

A useful cluster combines several dimensions:

- **Audience tension:** the fear, desire, frustration, identity conflict, or decision underneath the topic.
- **Dominant promise:** what changes for the reader or viewer.
- **Hook family:** contrarian claim, curiosity gap, mistake, result, confession, prediction, case study, or practical utility.
- **Structural approach:** argument, list, tutorial, transformation story, teardown, framework, comparison, or experiment.
- **Proof mode:** personal experience, example, data, authority, demonstration, or social evidence.
- **Format and platform:** where the pattern appears and whether the medium changes it.
- **Payoff:** the insight, action, or emotional resolution delivered.

Two posts mentioning the same topic can belong to different clusters if they solve different tensions. Two posts using different vocabulary can belong together if they make the same promise through the same structure.

## Evidence requirements

A market cluster should normally have repeated evidence from more than one post and more than one creator. Name representative sources, not every source. Record a counterexample or boundary when available. If only one strong example exists, label it an emerging example or hypothesis rather than a durable pattern.

## Assess saturation

Saturation is not simply the number of posts. Consider whether many creators use the same promise, hook, evidence, and conclusion. A busy topic can still contain a fresh audience, proof source, format, or point of view. Conversely, a small topic can feel saturated when every post repeats the same shallow claim.

Use qualitative labels such as low, moderate, high, or unclear and explain the reason. Look for language convergence, repeated examples, interchangeable advice, and diminishing differentiation. Do not infer audience exhaustion solely from creator repetition.

## Find the opening

For each cluster, ask:

1. What interest is proven?
2. What does the dominant content keep saying?
3. What question, audience, proof, consequence, or format remains weakly served?
4. What would make a contribution meaningfully different rather than cosmetically novel?
5. Does the owner possess relevant experience, evidence, or positioning?

The unclaimed gap should be phrased as a concrete content opportunity. "Be more original" is not a gap. "Show first-time managers how to run a weekly one-to-one on a real team problem, using the owner's documented cases" is actionable.

## Cluster output

Name the cluster in plain language. Include its audience tension, dominant promise, representative evidence, common hook and structure, saturation, counter-signal, unclaimed gap, and relevance to the owner. The cluster is an analytical container, not a claim that every source behaves identically.
