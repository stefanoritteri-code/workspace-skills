# Representative market corpus

> Reference for writing-pack-topic-research. Read when building the corpus or checking that it is representative.

A topic opportunity cannot be inferred from a handful of viral screenshots. Build a corpus that represents the conversation, the creators participating in it, and the posts that did not support the easy conclusion.

## Coverage targets

For a full Topic Opportunity Map, begin with roughly twenty to forty candidates across the selected research lanes. Structurally analyze twelve to twenty useful examples and fully read four to eight pivotal posts, videos, transcripts, or essays. These ranges are guides, not quotas. A narrow niche may support less; a broad market may need more. State when the evidence is thin.

Cover multiple creators. A repeated pattern from one account is a creator pattern, not yet a market pattern. Prefer several examples from different creators and include more than one post per important cluster when available. Avoid allowing a famous account's raw reach to dominate the result.

## Compare performance fairly

Prioritize posts that outperform each creator's own usual results. A post with modest raw engagement may be a strong signal for a smaller creator, while a large account's average post may not indicate unusual demand. You can only call something creator-relative when you have actually looked at that creator's other recent posts; otherwise report the raw numbers and say the baseline is unknown. Keep formats clean when the decision is format-specific: compare videos with videos, text posts with text posts, and long-form work with appropriate long-form work.

Record publication date, platform, format, relative performance when established, and source URL. Never invent missing metrics or convert incomparable platform numbers into fake precision.

## Include counter-signals

Winner-only research exaggerates certainty. Deliberately look for:

- ordinary or weak posts using the same promise as a winner;
- recent posts that contradict an older pattern;
- creators with a different audience for whom the pattern fails;
- strong ideas whose packaging or distribution appears weak;
- repeated formats that earn attention but little meaningful response;
- evidence that a supposed gap is already crowded under different language.

Counter-signals do not automatically invalidate a pattern. They define its boundary and improve the recommendation.

## Read before synthesizing

Search snippets and previews are not enough for pivotal claims. Fully read the examples that define a cluster, provide its strongest proof, or challenge the emerging conclusion. Distinguish what is visible in the source from your interpretation of why it worked.

## Corpus readiness check

Before calling the corpus representative, ask: Does it span the important lanes? Does it contain multiple creators? Are the strongest examples creator-relative outliers rather than merely famous accounts? Are dates and formats appropriate? Did we include counter-signals? Were pivotal sources actually read? If any answer is no, qualify the finding or continue researching.
