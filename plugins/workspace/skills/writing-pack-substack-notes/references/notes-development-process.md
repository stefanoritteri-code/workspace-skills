# Substack Notes development process

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-notes. Read when the shape of the whole job is unclear.

Substack Notes are not miniature newsletters and they are not tweets with a different destination. A useful Note creates one complete reading experience in the feed while also contributing to a longer relationship with the writer and publication. The unit of work is therefore a **Notes portfolio**, not a pile of interchangeable hooks.

## 1. Start with the reader change

Before drafting, name what the reader should understand, notice, feel, reconsider, or discuss. Keep this change small enough for one Note. A newsletter may carry a complete argument; a Note usually carries one observation, mechanism, reframe, scene, useful list, or question.

When the input is incomplete, ask for the smallest missing piece. Useful starting material includes a rough idea, a passage from a newsletter, a personal observation, a note on the owner's record, a reference Note, or a problem the publication repeatedly explores. Do not make the owner complete a long intake form.

## 2. Separate the evidence lanes

- **Owned material** supplies the writer's experience, claims, examples, worldview, and voice.
- **Market research** shows what readers are discussing, which promises are saturated, and what questions remain open.
- **Reference Notes** demonstrate structural mechanisms such as opening pressure, rhythm, specificity, progression, analogy, and ending.

References never supply the writer's identity or personal story. Borrow a mechanism, not language, persona, or conclusions.

## 3. Mine a portfolio of angles

Extract five to eight genuinely different angles before drafting. Each should offer a different reason to care. Then assign editorial roles so the batch works over time:

- two Discovery Notes that are immediately legible to a new reader;
- one Depth Note that teaches or develops an idea;
- one Voice Note that reveals taste, worldview, lived detail, or personality;
- one Publication Bridge that connects naturally to a real article or publication asset;
- one Conversation Note that invites a specific, meaningful response.

If there is no publication asset worth bridging to, replace that slot with another Depth or Voice Note. Never invent a link, CTA, or article.

## 4. Draft each Note as a complete object

Every Note must stand alone. It can be concise or expansive, but it should have one center of gravity. Use paragraphs, fragments, lists, or questions because the idea benefits from them, not to imitate a platform style. Default to no hashtags and no forced CTA.

Deliver the six Notes one by one, each under its editorial-role label, with the clean publishable text in a copyable block. Research explanation, source receipts, and editorial commentary stay outside the publishable body.

## 5. Revise the portfolio, not only the sentences

Check whether the batch varies in angle, opening, structure, emotional register, length, and ending. Remove duplicate theses disguised by formatting. Confirm that a new reader can understand the Discovery Notes, the Depth Note rewards attention, the Voice Note sounds owned, the bridge is earned, and the conversation prompt has a real answer space.

## 6. Deliver without assuming publication

The owner may keep individual Notes, keep the batch as a page, or revise selected Notes. After the portfolio is approved, offer a posting plan or tasks to post selected Notes. Never plan the whole batch by default. Preserve the exact approved text during handoff. Posting itself is always theirs.
