# Openings, endings, and conversation on Substack Notes

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-notes. Read when writing openings, endings and the Conversation Note.

The opening earns attention by beginning the idea, not by withholding it. The ending completes the Note or creates a meaningful opening for response. Both should fit the editorial role and the writer's real level of certainty.

## Opening families

- **Specific observation:** name a pattern the reader can immediately test against experience.
- **Direct recommendation:** tell the reader what to try, then earn the recommendation with reasoning.
- **Tension:** place two beliefs, behaviors, or outcomes beside each other so the contradiction becomes visible.
- **Scene or detail:** begin inside a concrete moment rather than explaining the entire context first.
- **Reframe:** state the common interpretation and the more useful alternative.
- **Analogy:** start with the familiar half of a comparison and quickly reveal the mapping.
- **Identity recognition:** name who may recognize themselves, then prove it through precise details.
- **Genuine question:** ask something narrow enough that good answers could disagree.

Do not require every opening to sound declarative, contrarian, urgent, or optimized. Warmth, uncertainty, curiosity, and understatement are valid when they match the material.

## Ending families

- **Payoff:** show what changes when the reader applies the idea.
- **Implication:** widen the meaning without introducing a new essay.
- **Image:** return to a concrete detail that now carries more significance.
- **Boundary:** state when the advice does not apply or what it cannot solve.
- **Next action:** give one practical experiment rather than a generic command.
- **Bridge:** offer the connected article as a deeper continuation after the Note already delivers value.
- **Question:** invite a specific answer that can improve the conversation.
- **Clean stop:** end on the completed claim. Not every Note needs a CTA.

## Conversation without engagement bait

A Conversation Note should create a useful answer space. Good prompts ask for an example, tradeoff, threshold, decision, or competing interpretation. The context before the question matters: readers should understand why the answer is worth giving.

Weak: "Do you agree?"

Stronger: "What is one habit that became easier only after you changed the environment around it?"

Weak: "Thoughts on AI in the studio?"

Stronger: "Which part of your process would you never hand to a machine, even if it did it better?"

Never disguise a predetermined conclusion as an open question. If the writer mainly wants to teach, use an implication or practical ending instead.

## Portfolio rhythm

Across six Notes, vary the first move and the landing. Two Notes can share a format only when their angles and emotional effects are materially different. Watch for repeated constructions, repeated rhetorical questions, identical line lengths, and the same CTA. A portfolio should feel like one recognizable person with more than one mode of thought.
