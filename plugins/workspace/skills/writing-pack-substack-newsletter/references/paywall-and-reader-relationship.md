# Paywall and reader relationship

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-newsletter. Read when paid publishing or the CTA is in play.

A paywall is an editorial boundary, not a suspense trick. Recommend one only when the publication has paid subscriptions or the owner explicitly wants help with free-to-paid conversion. A valuable free article can be the correct outcome.

## Decide whether a paywall belongs

Ask what the paid reader is paying for. Credible paid value often includes deeper analysis, an executable method, proprietary or carefully synthesized research, a case study, detailed examples, templates, exercises, decision support, or ongoing access. A generic second half, a list of obvious tips, or the answer promised by the title is not enough.

Useful options are:

- **Fully free:** best for reach, trust, a foundational idea, or when paid depth is not genuinely different.
- **Free premise, paid depth:** the free section establishes the tension and core insight; paid readers receive the full method, evidence, examples, or implementation.
- **Free framework, paid application:** the model is public; the paid section helps a reader use it through cases, prompts, exercises, or decisions.
- **Paid from the beginning:** appropriate only when the publication relationship and subject justify it. The public preview still needs an honest description of value.

## Choose the boundary

Place a paywall after a meaningful free unit, not in the middle of a sentence or immediately before the promised answer. The free reader should leave with a complete insight. The paid reader should receive a second, clearly stronger layer.

For the proposed boundary, state:

1. what the free reader receives;
2. what additional problem the paid section solves;
3. why the boundary belongs at that exact transition;
4. what promise introduces the paid section;
5. whether the current evidence and material can fulfill that promise.

If the answer to the fifth question is no, do not manufacture filler. Keep the article free or ask them for the missing material.

## Reader relationship

Substack is a repeated relationship, not a sequence of isolated conversion pages. Titles, openings, paywalls, CTAs, and publishing cadence train the reader's expectations. Protect trust by being specific about what is free, what is paid, what is known, and what remains uncertain.

Use the ending to complete the editorial job. A CTA may invite a thoughtful reply, a subscription, a paid upgrade, a related article, a product, or a next practice. It should follow naturally from what the reader just received. Do not attach the same subscribe or upgrade sentence to every article.

## Paywall audit

Before approval, verify that the title is fulfilled across the intended access boundary, the free section is not deliberately crippled, the paid section contains distinct value, transitions remain coherent, and the CTA respects the reader. The owner makes the final publishing and access decision. This skill can recommend and explain; it never silently inserts a paywall or publishes the article.
