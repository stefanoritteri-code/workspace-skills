# Reference articles — editorial mechanisms

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-newsletter. Read when a structure would benefit from a worked reference.

Four public Substack articles, each chosen because it demonstrates one editorial mechanism clearly. Their text is not kept here: when a reference matters, open the link and read it in full before leaning on it. They teach MECHANISM, never voice — borrow the movement, rebuild it from the owner's thesis and evidence, and never reuse phrases, metaphors, anecdotes, worldview, or cadence. Engagement figures are as shown when the set was assembled (2026), not a baseline.

| Article | Link | Mechanism it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| Gurwinder — "The Intellectual Obesity Crisis" (17/05/22) | https://www.gurwinder.blog/p/the-intellectual-obesity-crisis | **Conceptual diagnosis.** A coined concept carried by one sustained analogy (information as food) through the full arc: why the craving evolved, how the environment changed, the mechanism, the named condition, its consequences, then a practical response and a reflective close. The concept earns its place because it makes a diffuse problem visible. |
| Dan Koe — "How to articulate yourself intelligently" (20/11/25) | https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-articulate-yourself-intelligently | **Educational / mechanism-led.** Personal stakes first, then one central model, then progressively deeper frameworks layered on top of it, ending in practice. Study the progression from stake to model to depth. |
| Dan Koe — "If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years" (10/01/26) | https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/if-you-have-multiple-interests-do | **Identity recognition + contrarian argument.** Names the reader's guilt ("multiple interests is a weakness"), attacks the received sequence (specialisation), introduces the missing piece as a personal discovery, then numbered big ideas moving from why to practical steps. Worldview-led — do not inherit the worldview. |
| ixcarus — "Neuroplasticity: Rewiring Your Brain To Do Hard Things" (02/01/26) | https://ixcarus.substack.com/p/neuroplasticity-rewiring-your-brain | **Transformation explainer.** Felt struggle → accessible science mechanism → what changes for the reader → realistic actions. Watch for, and never copy, overstated science: keep evidence limits explicit. |

## How to use them

- Propose routes by mechanism, not author: "a conceptual-diagnosis issue built on your term for X" — not "a Gurwinder-style piece".
- Before drafting from a reference, write one line naming the borrowed mechanism in abstract terms; then put the reference away and build from their material.
- Originality test: remove the names — if the draft would still read as the reference's, change the argument or proof, not just the words.
