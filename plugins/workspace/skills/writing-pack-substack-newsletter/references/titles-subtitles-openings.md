# Substack titles, subtitles, and openings

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-newsletter. Read when building the package.

Packaging is a contract. The title and subtitle should earn the right reader's attention and accurately describe the value the article delivers. The opening should confirm that contract quickly enough to keep the reader moving.

## Build title families, not synonym lists

Generate five to seven options across different mechanisms:

- **Named concept:** gives a diffuse problem a memorable and defensible label.
- **Desired transformation:** states what the reader can understand or achieve.
- **Identity recognition:** helps a specific reader recognize that the piece is for them.
- **Contrarian correction:** challenges advice or a belief the article can actually disprove.
- **Question or investigation:** opens an uncertainty the article genuinely resolves.
- **Mechanism:** foregrounds the reason something happens, not merely the outcome.
- **Personal stake:** uses a real owned experience as the entry point.
- **Specific guide:** promises a bounded method, decision, or result.

Do not place a number in every title. Avoid false urgency, universal claims, unsupported superlatives, vague power words, fake confessions, and borrowed syntax that makes the article resemble its reference more than its author.

## Subtitle roles

A subtitle can narrow the audience, reveal the mechanism, clarify the scope, name the transformation, or add necessary context. It should not repeat the title in longer words. Read the pair as one unit. If either half can be removed without losing meaning, improve the weaker half.

## Opening mechanisms

Offer two openings with genuinely different jobs:

- a concrete scene that contains the tension;
- a direct statement of the reader's problem and the promised change;
- a surprising but supportable observation;
- a precise question with real consequences;
- a coined concept followed by a clean definition;
- a personal admission or result the owner actually owns;
- a familiar belief followed by the specific reason it fails.

An opening is not a theatrical hook pasted onto a different article. By the end of the opening, the reader should know what tension matters, why the writer cares or can help, and what kind of payoff is coming.

## Packaging workflow

1. Write the reader promise and thesis before generating titles.
2. Extract the article's most ownable assets: concept, result, tension, identity, mechanism, scene, evidence, and transformation.
3. Generate options across at least four title families.
4. Pair each with a subtitle that adds information.
5. Check every option against the actual outline and evidence.
6. Lead with the recommended primary package; give a one-line tradeoff for at most two alternates.
7. Recheck the title after the final draft. The article may have discovered a better center.

## Opening audit

Remove throat-clearing about wanting to write the piece, generic claims about a changing world, definitions the reader already knows, and suspense that withholds the central issue without reason. Preserve a slower opening when atmosphere or narrative creates the article's value. Compression is useful only when it strengthens clarity and voice.

The final title, subtitle, and opening should feel inevitable together. They should attract the correct reader, establish trust, and create forward motion without making a promise the body cannot keep.
