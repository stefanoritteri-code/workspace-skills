# Substack editorial workflow

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-newsletter. Read when diagnosing the starting point or when the order of work is unclear.

The outcome is one original, editable Substack article that makes a clear promise to a specific reader and fulfills it. The workflow is adaptive. It should not drag the owner through idea discovery when the owner has a strong draft, and it should not draft a polished-looking article when the thesis and evidence are still weak.

## 1. Diagnose the starting point

Classify the input before asking questions:

- **Blank page:** a publication, audience, or project but no idea. Search the owner's relevant material first (their idea notebook, journal and inbox, where they keep them), then offer a small set of defensible possibilities.
- **Rough idea:** a topic, observation, question, or claim. Clarify the reader change and test whether there is enough owned substance to support it.
- **Source-led:** notes, research, a saved post, PDF, transcript, or reference newsletter. Read the pivotal source before proposing an article.
- **Direction chosen:** the thesis and audience are clear. Build the editorial package and outline without repeating discovery.
- **Existing draft:** critique before rewriting. Find the three problems with the greatest effect on reader experience.

Ask only for information that changes the article. A good first question identifies the intended change: what should the reader understand, feel, believe, or do differently? Follow with audience, evidence, publication goal, or voice only when those are genuinely missing.

## 2. Establish the editorial brief

Capture a working brief in conversation:

- intended reader and their current tension;
- reader transformation;
- central thesis or question;
- owned evidence, stories, observations, and examples;
- external evidence that must be verified;
- publication goal and any paid-reader goal;
- voice, length, timing, and CTA constraints.

Do not invent missing personal material. Mark an evidence gap and ask for it, find a credible external source, or narrow the claim.

## 3. Research selectively

Retrieve the owner's material by relevance, not volume. Research the market when it answers a real decision: whether the angle is saturated, what readers already know, what title families are working, which claims need support, or what counterargument must be addressed. Separate their evidence, market evidence, and structural references. A popular reference can demonstrate a mechanism; it cannot donate its thesis, story, metaphor, or voice.

## 4. Choose direction before prose

When several directions are credible, present three routes, recommended route first, each as one short paragraph: the thesis, the proof it needs, and the risk. Useful families are educational mechanism, personal narrative, argument or contrarian essay, conceptual diagnosis, transformation guide, and cross-domain synthesis. If the owner has already made the important choice, proceed.

## 5. Package, outline, draft, revise

The default approval sequence is:

1. one-sentence promise and thesis;
2. five to seven title and subtitle pairs, recommended primary first;
3. two materially different opening approaches;
4. a section outline, one line per section: the claim and its proof (example, transition, and payoff are authoring criteria applied while writing, not outline fields);
5. optional paywall and CTA recommendations;
6. one complete draft;
7. high-leverage revision of the same draft.

The sequence is flexible. The owner can request a fast draft, titles only, an outline, or critique. Make assumptions explicit rather than making the process ceremonial.

## Definition of done

The finished article has an honest promise, an identifiable thesis, owned specificity, verified or clearly qualified claims, visible progression, original language, and an ending that changes or rewards the reader. It exists as one clean piece, not a trail of competing drafts. Research receipts remain inspectable outside the publishable body. Publishing or scheduling is the owner's own act, after their approval.
