# Substack article revision rubric

> Reference for writing-pack-substack-newsletter. Read when critiquing or revising a draft.

Revision should improve the article the owner chose, not replace it with a generic house style. Diagnose before rewriting. Start with the three changes that would most improve the reader experience, then preserve the draft's strongest ideas, scenes, phrases, and rhythms.

## 1. Promise and reader

- Can the intended reader recognize why this article is for them?
- Does the title and subtitle promise a real change?
- Does the article deliver that promise at the right depth?
- Is the scope narrow enough to be credible and useful?

## 2. Thesis and originality

- Is there one central claim, question, or transformation?
- Does the article have an owned point of view rather than assembled advice?
- Are references used for mechanism or evidence without donating language and identity?
- Could the thesis be stated clearly without erasing necessary nuance?

## 3. Opening

- Does the opening establish tension, stakes, and direction?
- Is any throat-clearing, generic scene-setting, or false suspense delaying the article?
- If the opening is slow, is atmosphere or narrative doing valuable work?
- Does it match the title rather than beginning a different essay?

## 4. Structure and progression

- Does every section move the argument, story, or transformation forward?
- Are claims, proof, examples, and implications sequenced logically?
- Are transitions earned rather than decorative?
- Is a list actually a progression, or merely several related observations?
- Does the ending resolve the opening promise?

## 5. Proof and specificity

- Are consequential facts verified and placed near the relevant claim?
- Are fact, inference, experience, and opinion distinguishable?
- Does the article include concrete scenes, examples, mechanisms, or decisions?
- Are counterarguments treated fairly?
- Has research added understanding rather than citation volume?

## 6. Voice and rhythm

- Does the article sound like the owner at their clearest?
- Are sentence length, paragraphing, transitions, and emphasis varied intentionally?
- Has compression removed personality, warmth, ambiguity, or needed explanation?
- Are repeated phrases purposeful rather than accidental?
- Is any passage imitating a reference creator's distinctive cadence?

## 7. Paid value and ending

- If there is a paywall, does the free section provide a complete insight?
- Does the paid section deliver materially deeper value?
- Is the CTA relevant to the article and publication goal?
- Does the ending land a consequence, decision, image, reframe, or next action rather than mechanically summarize?

## Revision order

Revise in this order: promise and thesis, evidence gaps, structural progression, opening and ending, section-level clarity, then sentence polish. Copyediting a weak argument first wastes effort. After revision, reread the title, subtitle, first paragraph, section headings, paywall boundary, and final paragraph as one compressed article. They should tell the same story.

Revise the existing piece; do not start a new draft for each pass. When a requested change affects only one section, preserve the rest. If the owner rejects a source or direction, remove its influence from both reasoning and prose before continuing.
