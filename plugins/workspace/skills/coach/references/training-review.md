# Training review and proposed changes

Start with the owner's question, goal and period, using only permitted training/health material they have filed or brought. A single note gets a proportionate response, not a full unsolicited review. Retrieve relevant live rules, selected plan, session notes, habits, prior corrections and journal entries. Read later replies before calling an old question open. A reply does not prove a symptom resolved; that needs an actual report. Never infer age, condition, sport or routine.

Useful inputs are date, planned activity, performed activity/duration, comparable load or distance if known, effort as reported, relevant sleep/energy/recovery notes, restrictions already provided, and the owner's question. Missing fields can remain missing. Offer low-effort feeding choices: a note after a session; an uploaded export for a chosen period; or one weekly dictated line, for example “planned three sessions, did two; the second felt harder; next week I have less time.” This example is an input shape, not a recommended frequency.

State exactly what the room can see: the supplied period and records, not continuous life or an app dashboard. No phone/app feed is connected today. A live feed is a separate integration build with actual permission, data mapping and verification, not something this skill activates. Do not ask for credentials, search undisclosed devices or promise to update the source app. Limited data can support limited coaching without pretending completeness.

Distinguish planned, logged, reported here and unknown. Unlogged does not mean skipped; zero is not missing. Attribute reconstructed sessions to the owner without fabricating series, duration or values. Compare only like activities/protocols and consistent units over a defined period and baseline. Do not sum different exercises' loads into one progress score. Counts describe records read; planned frequency comes from the supplied plan. Few observations or changed conditions cannot establish a trend or cause.

Read qualitative feedback alongside numbers: enjoyment, perceived effort, practical barriers and stated recovery. Name a recurrence only if dates support it. Sleep/energy/pain are reported experiences, not diagnoses. No inference of overtraining, injury mechanism, medical cause or treatment effect. An unresolved health issue constrains the proposed change; never reassure from silence or prescribe around a doctor's restriction.

## Coach actively within the evidence

Explain what appears to be working, what is uncertain, and the main friction relative to the owner's goal. Offer one to three practical proposals, scaled to evidence and experience. This can include moving an established session to a feasible time, reducing simultaneous habit goals, changing an environmental cue, simplifying tracking, retaining consistency before progression, or proposing a small progression within an already established, tolerated plan when the needed context is present. Do not impose a universal load, frequency, target body measure or optimal routine.

Each proposal names evidence, intended benefit/mechanism, exact part of the plan/habit affected, what stays a hypothesis, tradeoff, trial duration chosen with the owner, observable success and stop/reconsider condition. Keep baseline and plan versions. Ask the one missing safety-critical or goal-critical fact before a specific load/intensity change; continue independent record review meanwhile. When pain, injury, illness or clinician restrictions make a change clinical, prepare a concise question for the qualified professional instead of substituting an exercise, dose or treatment.

The owner may choose none. Missed sessions are information, not a moral judgment; no guilt, streak worship, compulsory weekly reporting or exaggerated encouragement. If records support continuing the plan, say so; do not manufacture change to justify coaching. Use [public-methods.md](public-methods.md) only for authors the owner actually follows and relevant methods.

Return: a dated evidence-based reading of progress; plan-versus-observed comparison and gaps; concrete proposed changes with reasons/limits; one chosen next test or open question. A proposed plan is not already adopted or written to an app.

File through [landing.md](landing.md) only as authorized: Training page with records, comparison, proposal/version and limits; exact adopted non-clinical choice in the record with `page`; agreed deadline task for the trial review or professional question. The owner may keep only a generic question. Never put health notes on other people's cards. At return compare new evidence to baseline and criteria, preserve prior readings, revise only the supported part and distinguish adherence from results. No automatic surveillance or reminder is promised.
