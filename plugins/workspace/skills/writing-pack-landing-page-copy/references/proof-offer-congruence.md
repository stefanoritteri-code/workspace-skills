# Proof, price, and congruence

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when placing proof, presenting price, stating the guarantee, or checking congruence.

The page's believability system. Claims create desire; proof converts it. A page whose promises outrun its proof leaks trust at every scroll.

## The proof hierarchy

From strongest to weakest: demonstration (show the thing working), specific named results with numbers and timeframes, testimonials from people like the reader answering a real objection, aggregate numbers (customers served, years running), borrowed authority (press, credentials), and bare assertion. Use the strongest proof available at each claim, and place proof NEXT to the claim it supports — a proof section quarantined at the bottom supports nothing. Where there is no proof for a claim, mark the gap plainly in chat and either soften the claim or leave a labeled placeholder; never fabricate a testimonial, statistic, or result, and never write "results not typical" copy around an invented number.

## Presenting price

Price arrives AFTER value is established — never in the headline or lead (except for most-aware pages, where the deal IS the message). Frame it against what it buys: the quantified outcome, the cost of the alternative (another year of the problem, the more expensive substitute), or the per-day arithmetic when honest. Then anchor it once and state it plainly; a price apologized for reads as a price not worth paying.

## Risk reversal on the page

The guarantee gets its own moment inside the CTA section, stated in the reader's terms: what exactly happens if it does not work for them, with the conditions in plain sight. A guarantee buried in footnote-speak reverses nothing. Restate it in one line beside the final button.

## Congruence

The page must repeat the promise the click made, and the checkout must repeat the promise the page made:

- **Traffic-source congruence:** the ad's or post's hook is the headline's starting point. A reader who clicked "the 15-minute method" and lands on a page about "transforming your business" bounces — the scent broke.
- **Internal congruence:** one page, one outcome, one CTA action. Competing offers or split CTAs ("buy now — or book a call — or download the guide") divide momentum; secondary actions belong on other pages.
- **Voice congruence:** the page sounds like the owner (or the client it is written for) at their most direct — read the voice rules first — not like a template with a name on it.

## Rules

- Every number on the page must come from the owner or their materials; every claim the owner must be able to substantiate is flagged when it cannot be.
- Urgency and scarcity claims obey the same truth test as everything else: literally true or cut.
