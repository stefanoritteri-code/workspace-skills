# Landing page delivery contract

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when delivering, saving or revising a page.

One page = one piece of work, revised in place. The chat carries the diagnosis, the proof-gap flags and the commentary; the page carries only the page.

## The deliverable

Title: `Landing page — <offer>`. The body is the page in its structural order, as markdown:

1. **Headline Variations** — numbered 1–3, each a distinct angle.
2. **Sub-headline Variations** — numbered 1–3.
3. The **Lead** — flowing copy, short paragraphs, bullets with air between them.
4. The **Transformation Section** — its headline as a markdown heading, then the copy.
5. **[TESTIMONIAL SECTION PLACEHOLDER]** — verbatim, with one line beneath it specifying what proof belongs there (from whom, answering which objection).
6. The **Offer Introduction** — its headline as a heading, then the benefit-led presentation.
7. The **Call to Action** — transformation contrast, price, risk reversal, button text.

Mark any claim awaiting proof with *(needs proof)* inline so the gaps are visible in the draft itself. Keep the diagnosis, editing notes and alternatives beyond the specified variations OUT of the page.

## Where it lives

- Always: the full page in the chat.
- In the room: on the owner's yes, a page in their library via `write_page` (`author: counterpart`, title as above). Revisions are dated `append`s to the same page, never a second page.
- Terminal: the brain's notebooks, under `ideas-lab/landing-page/YYYY-MM-DD-<offer-slug>.md`, revised in place.

## Revising

"Punch up the lead" means the lead changes inside the existing page — deliver the full revised page, never a second draft of the same page. A genuinely different page (different offer, different traffic source, different tier) is a new piece; say which one you are working in when more than one exists.

## Working from the Offer Blueprint

When an Offer Blueprint exists, it is the source of truth for the promise, mechanism, guarantee, and stack — search for it by title (`Offer Blueprint — <product>`) before asking anything. If page-writing reveals a weakness in the offer itself (a promise with no proof, a guarantee that cannot be stated plainly, no mechanism to satisfy a sophisticated market), say so in chat and offer the `writing-pack-offer-design` handoff rather than papering over it with copy.

## After delivery

Ask in chat — outside the page — which sections to revise or which headline direction to develop. When the owner picks a headline variation, fold it into the page as THE headline on the next revision and drop the losing variants. When the owner approves or rejects the page, record which and why (see "Where the work lands").
