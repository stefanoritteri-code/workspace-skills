# Page anatomy and length tiers

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when building the skeleton or setting the length tier.

The page's fixed skeleton. The order is not decorative — each section exists to move a specific reader state to the next one — and the diagnosis (awareness, sophistication) decides how much weight each section carries.

## The sections, in order

1. **Main Headline** — 3 variations, each a genuinely different angle (big idea, problem, timeframe, mechanism), never three phrasings of one angle. Written for the diagnosed awareness stage.
2. **Sub-headline** — 3 variations that extend the headline with compelling angles it did not cover: the proof tease, the qualifier, the mechanism if the headline led with the promise.
3. **Lead** — problem amplification per the Halbert method: enter the reader's existing conversation, sharpen the cost of inaction, bullets with air between them. Length scales with awareness (unaware traffic needs a long lead; product-aware traffic almost none).
4. **Transformation Section** — with its own headline hinting at the change. A vivid before-and-after (the owner's own, a client's, or the reader's projected one — labeled honestly), with the unique mechanism introduced as the HOW of the transformation. This is where mechanism-level sophistication gets satisfied.
5. **Testimonial Section** — insert a clear [TESTIMONIAL SECTION PLACEHOLDER]. Never write testimonial copy; specify instead what kind of proof belongs there (who it should be from, what objection it should answer).
6. **Offer Introduction** — with its own headline stating the desired outcome. Features translated into benefits, one per line, each feature paired with what it does for the reader. The Offer Blueprint's stack maps here directly.
7. **Call to Action** — the transformation contrast (where they are now against where they could be), then price, then risk reversal. The CTA button text names the outcome, not the transaction ("Start landing retainers," not "Submit").

Medium and long pages repeat short CTAs after the transformation and offer sections; the reader who is ready should never have to scroll hunting for the button.

## Length tiers

- **Short (under 400 words):** newsletter opt-ins and free lead magnets. Headline, compressed lead, three to five benefit bullets, CTA.
- **Medium (750–1500 words):** typical products and services, warm traffic. The full skeleton at moderate depth.
- **Long (1500–3000 words):** cold or unaware audiences, premium prices, skeptical markets. Full skeleton with a long lead and layered proof.

The tier is set with the owner before drafting. When the requested tier conflicts with the diagnosis (a short page for cold traffic at a premium price), say so in one line, then write what the owner asked for.

## Rules

- Every section headline must work in the skimmer's chain: headline → subheads → CTA should read as a complete argument on its own.
- Nothing enters the page that the diagnosis did not call for — a section kept "because the template has it" is padding.
