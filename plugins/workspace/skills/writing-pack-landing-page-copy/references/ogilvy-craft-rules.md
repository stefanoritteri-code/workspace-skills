# Ogilvy's craft rules for the page

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when writing headlines, cutting adjectives, or structuring for skimmers.

David Ogilvy's discipline, distilled for landing pages: advertising is salesmanship in print, the reader is intelligent, and facts presented with specificity outsell cleverness presented with style.

## The headline carries the money

Most visitors read the headline and nothing else — the headline is worth the majority of the page's effort, which is why the page demands three real variations. A working headline does at least one of these: promises the reader a benefit they want, delivers news ("new," "now," "finally" — only when true), names the reader so the right person self-selects ("for freelance designers who…"), or provokes specific curiosity that the lead immediately pays off. A headline that is clever but unclear loses to one that is clear but plain, every time. Never write a blind headline that could sit atop any product in the category.

## Specificity is the style

- Exact numbers beat rounded claims: "helped 312 freelancers" outsells "helped thousands." A suspiciously precise fact reads as true; a round superlative reads as advertising.
- Facts beat adjectives. Cut "amazing," "revolutionary," "game-changing" on sight and replace each with the fact that would have earned it. If no fact exists, the claim goes.
- The reader is not a fool. Write as if to one intelligent friend who is skeptical of being sold — no talking down, no hollow enthusiasm, no exclamation-point energy.

## Long copy sells — when it is all substance

The more a purchase costs in money or trust, the more the page must say. Long copy outperforms short copy for considered purchases, but only when every line informs or advances desire; length from padding kills. The length tiers (short for opt-ins, medium for typical offers, long for cold traffic) implement this: cold, expensive, or skeptical situations earn the long tier.

## Structure for the skimmer

Most readers skim before they read. Subheads must tell the page's story on their own — a skimmer reading only headline, subheads, and CTA should still get the complete argument. Keep paragraphs short, front-load every section's first sentence, and caption anything image-like, because captions get read.

## Rules

- Every device on the page (headline form, proof, structure) serves the sale — remove anything that only serves the writer's ego.
- Truth constraint: nothing goes on the page the owner could not defend to the customer face to face. If a claim needs a qualifier to be true, write the qualifier.
