# Market sophistication and the escalation ladder

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when choosing the page's lead type.

Awareness asks where the reader starts; sophistication asks how tired the market is. Schwartz's second diagnostic: how many versions of this promise has the reader already heard? Every repetition of a claim weakens it, and the page must meet the market at its current level of exhaustion — a fresh market rewards the direct claim, a burned one punishes it.

## The five levels

1. **First to market** — nobody has made the claim before. The simple direct promise wins: state the outcome plainly and big. Almost no modern market is here.
2. **Second wave** — competitors say the same thing. The claim must be enlarged or sharpened: more specific, faster, bigger — but honest. Specificity beats superlatives ("312 freelancers" beats "thousands of people").
3. **Mechanism** — the enlarged claims have all been made and no longer land. The page leads with HOW: a named, distinctive mechanism that explains why this works when everything else the reader tried did not. The Unique Mechanism from the Offer Blueprint is built exactly for this level.
4. **Mechanism fatigue** — every competitor has a named system too. The mechanism must be enlarged or made easier: faster to work, less effort, works even in the hard cases ("even if you have no audience"). Proof density matters more than promise size here.
5. **Exhausted** — the reader no longer believes any promise in the category. Claims stop working entirely; the page leads with identification instead — the reader's identity, their felt experience, the feeling of being seen — and lets the product enter late, softly. Story-led and belief-led pages live here.

## Diagnosing the level

Ask what the reader has already seen: the competitors the owner named, the promises in the niche's ads and inboxes, what their prospects say they are skeptical of. If every competitor shouts the same outcome, the market is at least level 3; if every competitor also has a branded system, level 4. When the owner cannot say, default cautiously high (3–4) — leading with mechanism and proof never insults a fresh market, but a naked claim insults a burned one.

## Rules

- Sophistication picks the page's LEAD TYPE; awareness picks its starting point. The two diagnoses are stated together, in two lines, before the copy.
- Never respond to a saturated market with a bigger version of the saturated claim — escalate the TYPE of appeal (claim → mechanism → proof → identity), not the volume.
- When the requested angle sits below the market's level, say so in one line, then write what the owner asked for.
