# Prospect awareness on the landing page

> Reference for writing-pack-landing-page-copy. Read when diagnosing where the reader starts.

Schwartz's core diagnostic, applied to pages: where does the reader's mind start when the page loads? The awareness stage decides what the HEADLINE and LEAD must open with — not what the product is. A page that assumes too much awareness talks past the reader; one that assumes too little bores the ready buyer into closing the tab.

## The five stages, page by page

1. **Unaware** — the reader does not feel the problem. The page cannot open with the problem or the product; it opens with the READER: a story, an identity claim, or an observation so resonant it earns the right to introduce the problem. Longest pages, hardest traffic.
2. **Problem-aware** — the pain is felt, solutions unknown. The headline names the problem more precisely than the reader can name it themselves; the lead agitates honestly, then reveals that a way out exists. Most cold-traffic landing pages live here.
3. **Solution-aware** — the reader knows the category (courses exist, coaches exist, tools exist) but not this product. The headline leads with the outcome plus the mechanism — why THIS route works when the routes they have already dismissed did not.
4. **Product-aware** — the reader knows the product and has not bought. The page leads with differentiation, proof, and objection handling; the lead can be short because attention is already granted.
5. **Most-aware** — the reader wants it and needs a reason to act now. The page is nearly all offer: the deal, the deadline, the guarantee, the button. Copy length collapses as awareness rises.

## Diagnosing from what the owner gives you

- The traffic source is the biggest clue: cold social traffic is stage 1–2; search traffic on a category keyword is stage 3; retargeting and email-list traffic is stage 4–5. The same product needs different pages per source — say so when the owner wants one page for everything.
- The Offer Blueprint's Target Audience section carries the diagnosed level; trust it unless the traffic source contradicts it, and surface the contradiction if it does.
- When it is genuinely ambiguous, ask one question about where the visitor comes from — not a research interview.

## Rules

- One page, one stage. A page that walks a stone-cold reader all the way to a premium checkout in one visit fails at both ends; recommend a two-step (opt-in first) instead.
- The stage sets the opening; the offer's strength sets the close. A stage-2 page may honestly close on "get the free guide" rather than the sale.
- State the diagnosed stage in the two-line diagnosis before the copy so the owner can correct it cheaply.
